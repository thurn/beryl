Description

Read first: docs/beryl-3d.md 'The table' ('Verbs', 'Timing a script') and 'Determinism' (the two cases of changing targets). For behavior, read rust/crates/beryl_session/src/script/mod.rs (Script, beats, Step), script/act.rs (Act) and script/verb.rs, then ui/src/stage/playback.ts and tracks.ts for how beats and steps are ordered and when a step ends. The motion bead gave pose_at, Track and beat; the table bead gave placement.

In rust/beryl3d/crates/beryl_scene, src/play and src/verbs:

  • play::start(script, timings: &[StepTiming], now) compiles the whole script when it starts. For each beat, beat lays its steps out, and each step's verb turns it into segments at absolute times on the scene objects it concerns. Objects whose place the beat changed but that no step names (the rest of a hand) slide to their new place in 165 ms. When the script ends, every object is at its placement for the view.
  • It takes the timings as a slice. The bead that plays recorded fixtures passes script.timing. Do not read script.timing here, do not read a verb's duration from data, and do not port caseOf or timingOf.
  • Scripts never overlap: an update that arrives while a script plays waits, and takes effect when the script ends. When the window changes shape during a flight, a segment in progress keeps its start and duration and takes the new end pose, and its start pose is recomputed so that the pose at the present time is unchanged.
  • Verbs fly, appear and set, and the reset. Every other verb is an instant change that still holds its beat for the duration its StepTiming gives, logged once per verb as not yet built.
  • fly's arc is the first value in data/beryl3d/space.json: add "verbs": {"fly": {"arc": 55}}, in millimetres, read through the build script's constants.
  • When more than 10 updates are waiting, the script clock runs twice as fast, and past 40, four times. Put the two thresholds in space.json under catch_up.
  • wake() returns Now while a segment is active and At(t) before a future start.

Tests in beryl_scene/tests, on scripts and timings built by hand with the testing builders: frames at times t1 < t2 computed directly equal the same frames computed after stepping through every tick between; after a fly script ends, every object's pose equals its placement for the new view; an update arriving mid-script plays after it; a resize in mid-flight leaves the pose at that instant unchanged and the card arrives at its new placement; frame allocates nothing in a frame that starts a script.

Run: just b3d cargo-test -p beryl_scene; just b3d lane.

What the prototype found (rust/prototypes/beryl3d, 2026-11-14; read its code for a working example, then build it properly):

  • The prototype compiles a script's whole timeline when it starts, which gave 'positions are functions of time' with no special cases. See core/play.rs.
  • Verbs the client draws as an instant change still hold their beat for their data's duration (dwell 800 ms, announce 1,200 ms), or the game outruns the eye.
  • With a stand-in agent answering the human seat, the session ran far ahead of the animation (187 updates queued after 5 s), which is what the catch-up rule is for.

Decisions and deviations:

  • Scripts are compiled in handle time (Show::push / Show::resize), not in play::start at frame time: compiling places every intermediate table, which allocates, and the bead also requires that a frame that starts a script allocates nothing. Compiled times are on the script's own clock from 0; Show::frame starts a script at the frame's now, or exactly at the previous script's end when one was waiting. The entry point is play::Show (push(update, timings), resize, frame, wake, pose, next_unbuilt).
  • Objects no step names whose resting place a step changed slide over that step's own interval (or [opens.at, opens.by] when its act went ahead, or [to, to + closes] when it closes room), easing out, instead of a fixed 165 ms: the acceptance says nothing in src/play or src/verbs reads a duration from anywhere but a StepTiming, and the web's REGROUP (170 ms) is a constant in tracks.ts, not data.
  • Intermediate tables come from replaying the steps over beryl_session's script::Table and writing its objects, battlefield, stack, libraries and graveyards back into a PlayerView for placement. A step the table rejects changes nothing; the table takes the final view as it stands when the script ends.
  • Resets, the first view and scripts whose timings don't match their step count move nothing: their view shows as the script starts and the beats then only hold their time.
  • Unbuilt verbs are reported once each through Show::next_unbuilt (a bitmask); the scene has no log of its own, so the host logs them.
  • Client still pushes each update with no timings (so it shows every view at once) and still opens the prompt immediately. Passing script.timing (which landed on staging during this bead) and holding the prompt until its script ends are the recorded-fixtures bead's glue (by-q44s.1.38).
