Kernel answers players, opponents_of, next_player, turn and seat orders and defending players; Seat::opponent lives only in beryl_kernel::two_player, held by the two-player check; counts 404,620,798 -> 404,333,932

Description

Milestone 3 (plan K7, "Kernel rules"): "Players are reached through sets and orders: opponents of a player, players in turn order, defending players. The two-player shortcut is not exposed." No multiplayer logic is written: with two players every answer is what it is today.

Read first

docs/project-50k.md: Decisions K6, K7, K11 and K12, and "Crates", "State" and "Kernel rules" under Target architecture. rust/crates/beryl_kernel/src/lib.rs: its rustdoc is the kernel's API contract (who writes, mutator and getter naming, events, the board door, the legacy accessor, sets and orders); follow it, and change it in the same push when the code proves it wrong. enact brief shows the handoffs of the beads this one depends on.

Also read the handoffs of the by-w8gv.5.7 (players and turn) bead (Player and Turn are private; where the per-player arrays live) and the by-w8gv.5.12 (Whose) bead (the functions where card data meets the shortcut).

Scope

  • The API (contract, section h): opponents of a player (a player set), each player in turn order starting from the active player or from a given one (APNAP, CR 101.4), the next player in turn order (for passing priority, CR 117.3d, and for whose turn comes next), the defending player or players of an attacker (CR 506.2), every player. The player set is the kernel's (from interpret/scene.rs's Who; the by-w8gv.5.6 (order-free sets) bead moves it, or move it here if that bead has not landed).
  • Seat::opponent (beryl_card/src/ids.rs:44) call sites when this was decomposed. beryl_engine/src, 21: cast.rs:117, :322; combat.rs:23, :686, :694; effect.rs:1207, :1323; engine.rs:650, :695; event.rs:819; filter.rs:93; setup.rs:48, :77, :82, :298; start.rs:192, :201; turn.rs:20, :45; interpret/scene.rs:46, :231. Elsewhere, 31: beryl_session (protocol.rs 3, game.rs 2, checkpoint.rs 2, save.rs 1, tests 4), beryl_search (rollout.rs 3, expert/ 4, frozen/ 7), beryl_cli (fixture.rs 2, blunders.rs, review.rs, longthink/term.rs), beryl_cards (blue/timetwister.rs, white/beza_the_bounding_spring.rs). Engine tests have about 40 more.
  • Each site says which question it asks: "the player who wins when this one loses" (effect.rs:1323, event.rs:819: with more players nobody wins yet, CR 104.2a; keep it as "the game ends if one player is left"), "the defending player" (combat.rs), "the next player to get priority" (engine.rs:695), "the non-active player" (turn.rs), "each opponent" (scene.rs:231, the Whose functions). Replace each with the API call that asks that question.
  • A UI or AI caller that really means "the other seat at this two-player table" (beryl_session's protocol and saves, beryl_search's evaluation, beryl_cli's fixtures) calls the two-player module by name. That is the "two-player layout" of the plan: one module, in the kernel, whose rustdoc says it is the only place the shortcut lives and what a third player would break. beryl_search/src/expert and frozen/ are frozen: point them at that module and change nothing else.
  • Seat::BOTH, Seat::P0/P1 and [_; 2] in rules modules (about 35 lines in beryl_engine/src when this was decomposed: start.rs 5, sba.rs 5, cast.rs 4, event.rs 3, invariants.rs 3 and others): iterate the API's "every player" instead. The arrays themselves are the by-w8gv.5.14 (capacities) bead's.
  • Seat stays in beryl_card (the M2 gate settled that) and stays a two-variant enum; do not redesign it. Removing opponent from its public surface may need the method to move to the kernel as a function over Seat, since beryl_card cannot hide it from one crate and show it to another.

Constraints

  • No N-player logic: no third seat, no "next opponent" choice, no range of influence. Where the rules for more players differ (who wins, who defends, turn order after a player leaves), cite the rule in the function's doc and implement the two-player case.
  • engine.rs's priority loop is hot. Run just bench-count before and after and record both.

Parity and checks

Parity (plan K11) at the push: just check passes; the frozen save corpus replays (cargo test -p beryl_session --test corpus); rollouts_never_change holds (cargo test -p beryl_bench rollouts_never_change); slow CI's counts lane (just count-gate, bar 405,276,238 instructions, 1% tolerance, rust/crates/beryl_bench/count-gate.toml) passes with no bump to the bar. No behavior changes: no rules fix, no new card, no new mechanic. Work the card-names and file-length allowlists down as files are touched (data/card-names-allowlist.json, data/file-length-allowlist.json; an entry moves with its file and its count only falls; a new file is under 800 lines). Rules code cites the Comprehensive Rules (AGENTS.md); keep the citations of code you move.

Acceptance criteria

beryl_kernel offers opponents of a player, players in turn order from a given player (CR 101.4), the defending players of a combat (CR 506.2) and the one player set; Seat::opponent is not callable from beryl_engine's rules modules, beryl_cards, beryl_search (outside expert/ and frozen/), beryl_session or beryl_cli: it lives on in one two-player module the kernel names; Seat::BOTH, Seat::P0 and Seat::P1 appear in beryl_engine only in tests and that module's callers the bead lists; Who::EVERYONE and its turn order come from the kernel; a comment on the bead lists every site left that assumes two players and why; parity holds.

Sites left that assume two players, and why (staging 27577e82). "5.14" = the per-player arrays and player count that by-w8gv.5.14 makes layout parameters.

THE SHORTCUT. beryl_kernel/src/two_player.rs: trait TwoPlayerSeat gives Seat::opponent(). Seat (beryl_card) no longer has the method, so .opponent() compiles only in a module that imports the trait. The two-player check (tools/src/beryl_tools/two_player.py, names lane) refuses the import in beryl_engine/src (outside lib.rs's re-export and tests/) and beryl_cards/src (outside tests).

Importers (grep TwoPlayerSeat), all of which mean "the other seat at this table":

  • beryl_kernel: players.rs (next_player), player_set.rs (in_turn_order: [first, the other]), board.rs (test door: begin_at and begin_before_turn count turns by alternating).
  • beryl_engine/src/tests: cast, combat, effects, events, game, mulligan, rules.
  • beryl_session: protocol/build.rs (a view's "opponent"), protocol/candidates.rs ("Attack <player>": Opt::Defender(None) names no player), protocol/outcome.rs and game.rs (the loser beside the winner; the second view of a review), checkpoint.rs (a hand card "shown" to the other seat); tests asks.rs, game_over.rs.
  • beryl_search: rollout.rs (the side a target should be on; the opponent's life for an attack), expert/ and frozen/ (frozen: one import line each, nothing else changed).
  • beryl_cli: fixture.rs (the agent in the seat the human is not), blunders.rs, review.rs, longthink/term.rs.

KERNEL, behind the format-neutral API (each rustdoc cites the rule more players change):

  • players.rs next_player: the other seat. More players: the next seat still in the game (CR 800.4a).
  • players.rs defending_player(attacker): an attacker records the planeswalker it attacks and no player, so one attacking a player attacks the sole defending player. More: the declaration must record which (CR 802.2a).
  • turn.rs pass_priority (passes < 2) and invariants.rs: all players have passed after two passes. 5.14's player count.
  • PlayerSet is a u8 built from the players array's length; in_seat_order walks Seat::BOTH. 5.14.
  • Per-player arrays [_; 2]: State::players and knowledge, Zones::members/library/graveyard, Resolving::knowledge, State::knowledge()/restore_knowledge, GameTable::decks, Profile::step_stops and hides_pointless. 5.14.
  • Seat::BOTH inside the kernel (new.rs, stack.rs, invariants.rs, chance.rs, object.rs, zone.rs, state.rs, observation.rs, knowledge.rs, table.rs, order.rs): walks of those arrays. 5.14.
  • Seat itself (beryl_card ids.rs): a two-variant enum with BOTH, P0, P1 (settled at the M2 gate; not redesigned).

ENGINE rules modules (no Seat::BOTH, P0, P1 or opponent() left outside tests):

  • combat.rs defender(): defending_players().sole().expect(..). The declarations are two-player: Opt::Defender(None) is "the" defending player, attack_targets lists one player's planeswalkers, block_decision has one player declare every block. More: CR 802.2, 802.4a.
  • combat.rs prune: no permanent records who controlled it when declared; a blocker or attacked planeswalker is removed when its controller is no defending player, which misses a change from one defending player to another.
  • sba.rs end(): any loss ends the game, the one player left winning (CR 104.2a) or a draw (104.4a). More: a loss that leaves two or more ends nothing. win_game makes every opponent lose (104.2b); end_game (concede) the same ending.
  • sba.rs: causes [Option<LossCause>; 2] and the legend rule's [Bitboard; 2] by controller; resolve.rs rewind's knowledge [Bitboard; 2]. 5.14.
  • engine.rs PlayOrDraw: Opt::Play / Opt::Draw is the two-player choice; "draw" is next_player. More: the chooser names any player (CR 103.1).

CARDS: none calls the shortcut. white/winds_of_abandon.rs keeps a [Bitboard; 2] by controller (5.14). It and azorius/fractured_identity.rs now walk players in turn order; with two players exactly one player acts in each, so nothing changed.

OUTSIDE THE RULES, not converted and not in this bead's acceptance: Seat::BOTH / P0 / P1 and [_; 2] in beryl_session (checkpoint.rs, protocol), beryl_search (eval.rs, mcts.rs, long.rs), beryl_cli (blunders.rs, evalfit.rs, transcript.rs), beryl_scenario (run.rs, script.rs, setup.rs: scenarios are written for P0 and P1).

Counts (just bench-count, rollouts): 404,620,798 before -> 404,333,932 after (-0.07%), measured before the rebase over by-w8gv.5.9. Corpus replay, rollouts_never_change and corpus_never_changes pass after it.

Deviations from the bead: (1) the shortcut is a trait method, not a free function, so the frozen expert code changed by one import line per file and every caller is found by one grep; (2) added the two-player check and an AGENTS.md line, because rustc suggests the import to anyone who writes .opponent(); (3) PlayerSet::EVERYONE and each_opponent are gone: State::players() and opponents_of() answer; filter::is_an_opponent is player != you; (4) added State::players_in_seat_order (decision candidates, events of one simultaneous happening, the one random stream: what saves and rollouts see), who_knows and in_zone (the engine's cast::in_zone, moved).
