Description

The next set of cards Beryl supports, chosen to settle the rules engine's hardest architecture problems before thousands of cards depend on the answers. The plan is docs/rules-coverage.md, revised 2026-11-13 in a design interview: sixteen decisions (R1 to R16), the keystone cards and their partners, and a proposed design for each of eight groups of work. Every bead is written against it.

What is built

24 keystone cards and about 25 partner cards, with the engine mechanisms they need: full layers with dependencies, one pipeline for replacement and prevention effects, characteristics in every zone, text changes, copiable values, the total-cost order, abilities on the stack as targets, merged and phased-out permanents, and a decision protocol in which one player may answer for another and a pick may be hidden.

Decisions taken with the user (2026-11-13)

  • About 20 of the hardest cards and the partners their scenarios need, not complete decks. The 24 were agreed as listed in the plan. A text-changing effect is in (Mind Bend); the DanDân format is a later target, so Dandân and Crystal Spray are its partners and nothing may assume a library or graveyard has one owner.
  • Absent cost: the existing count gate, never raised for a new mechanism. It stops the run.
  • Present cost: each step of a family's rollouts within 1.25 times the same step on the same state with the family removed (changed 2026-11-13, after the user asked what happens when a card changes the game a rollout plays: rollouts with and without a family play different games, so only a step from one state isolates the mechanism). A miss after three attempts is recorded and files a follow-up; it stops nothing.
  • Playable, not polished: every keystone plays in the app from both seats with existing prompt shapes and animation verbs. No Arena matching, no new animation, no AI strength bar.
  • Review: one Warden per group, a Mason after the three foundations and one at the end, one Archivist, no Sage.
  • Models by name: tier:opus for foundations, keystones and every review, tier:sonnet for partner cards and measurements. Nothing is tier:fable: the designs in the plan and in each bead come from the one Fable session that planned this. A bead filed later follows the same policy.

Defaults recorded without asking

  • Mandatory loops are a rules draw found in the engine; the "repeat N times" shortcut is designed in the plan and not built.
  • The State<Small> limit stays 20,480 bytes; the last-known-information table of group 1 frees the room the rest needs.
  • Subgames, restarts, banding, wishes and shared zones are designed in the plan and not built.
  • Phyrexian mana and modal spells are not in the vocabulary, so Quicksilver Gargantuan stands in for Phyrexian Metamorph and no partner is modal.

Order

Groups 1, 2 and 3 are foundations and land in that order. The Mason audit of the foundations gates groups 4 to 8, which then run in parallel. Edges are on the beads that need a result.

Whole-run acceptance

  • Every keystone and partner in the plan's table is implemented, with every clause of its Oracle text, and passes just card check.
  • The classic interactions listed under "Bars and validation" in the plan are scenarios that pass on Beryl and agree with XMage and Forge or are recorded divergences.
  • The count gate was never raised for a mechanism. Each family's present-cost number is within 1.25 times or recorded with its cause.
  • just fuzz plays games containing each family with no invariant failure and no stall other than a budget.
  • The plan's "Where the engine stands" table is rewritten by the Archivist to what shipped.

  • rust/crates/beryl_rules/src/: the rules. lib.rs is its contract. layers.rs, event.rs (the pipeline), trigger.rs, drawing.rs, damage.rs, sba.rs, combat.rs, cast/, mana/, engine.rs with engine/questions.rs, choices.rs, interpret/, rule_change.rs, tests/.
