The second half of Project 50k: an engine structure and a card workflow that would still work with a full implementation of the Comprehensive Rules and 50,000 card definitions. The plan is docs/project-50k-part-2.md, decided 2026-11-15 in a design interview: sixteen decisions (P1 to P16), the measurements behind them, and a proposed design for each group of work (Designs A to I). Every bead is written against it.

What is built

  • The keystone audit. Every Scryfall ruling of the 49 cards of by-bajq is a scenario or a recorded reason, held by a check (data/rulings-ledger.json), and a fuzz and exhaustive-search pool of every keystone runs in the nightly.
  • Guards. No wildcard match arm and no lossy integer cast in the kernel, rules and card crates; slot order cannot reach a mutation; every scenario plays in both state layouts; object references are typed by what they point at.
  • Rule ownership and one pass a module. Each engine module declares the Comprehensive Rules it implements, a check holds its citations to them, and just citations coverage lists the rules nobody owns. Each module is moved, converted to typed references and simplified in one pass.
  • The card workflow. Mutants for every part of a definition, a generator that reaches more, a card with no hand-written scenarios when generated ones kill every mutant and agree with both oracles, shared prelude functions for recurring compositions, a brief that drafts, just set plan, and a rewritten card-writing skill, proven on the existing cards and twelve new ones.
  • Compile and check time measured per crate as a series, the three largest costs reduced, and per-card tests run on a sample with the whole pool in the nightly.
  • 29 new keystone cards in three families: turn and game structure (Format, the turn as a plan, Time Stop, Paradox Haze, Relentless Assault, Time Vault, Karn Liberated, Shahrazad); casting and combat exceptions (casting inside a resolution, Bloodbraid Elf, Panglacial Wurm, Knowledge Pool, combat::assigner, Benalish Hero, Master of the Hunt); card shapes, face-down cards and cards outside the game (Fire // Ice, Wear // Tear, Bonecrusher Giant, Adventurous Eater, Valakut Awakening, Delver of Secrets, Erayo, Gisela, The Eldest Reborn, Wizard Class, Case of the Crimson Pulse, Student of Warfare, Arcane Proxy, Exalted Angel, Illusionary Mask, Burning Wish, Lurrus of the Dream-Den).

Decisions taken with the user (2026-11-15)

  • Oracle text is not parsed. Measured over the card store: 66% of cards have a sentence no other card has. Cards stay hand-written Rust files (P1).
  • A simple card needs zero hand-written scenarios when the checks pass (P2), and a card file cites the rules only for card-specific rulings (P3).
  • The workflow is built and proven on about a dozen cards. No set is run here; a Theros run is a later epic (P4).
  • Three keystone families are built, 29 cards as listed in the plan. The monarch, the initiative, day and night and voting are left to set work (P5).
  • The by-bajq cards are audited by a rulings ledger and a keystone pool, not by lockstep games against the oracles (P6).
  • Type safety goes as far as typed object references, with the contract landed first (P7).
  • Files are organised by rule ownership with a check; the 800-line limit stays (P8).
  • Shared card helpers are prelude functions that compose existing vocabulary. Accept parameters for details that vary by card, and keep readable expressions such as draw(you(), 3) at the call site (P9, revised 2026-11-16).
  • The shape of a game is one Format value; no other mode is built (P10).
  • One measured pass a module: move, typed references, simplify. No line count is a gate (P11).
  • by-bajq's closing Mason audit and its Archivist were closed unrun and folded in here: the baseline Mason (first bead of milestone 1) and this epic's Archivist do their work.

Defaults recorded without asking

  • No UI bar: new keystones are held to scenarios, oracles, fuzz and the script checks with existing verbs and prompt shapes. Nothing is built in the web UI or Beryl 3D (P13).
  • The performance bars of by-bajq carry over: the count gate is never raised for a mechanism and blocks the bead after three unsuccessful attempts without stopping independent work; a family's rollout step stays within 1.25 times the same step without it, and a miss is recorded and files a follow-up; State<Small> stays within 20,480 bytes; subgames live only in Large (P14).
  • Compile time is measured before anything is changed for it, and an engine change tests a sample of cards fixed by the commit with the whole pool in the nightly (P12).
  • Ante is designed and not built. Dexterity cards, un-cards, Alchemy cards and cards that only matter in a draft are out of scope. Omen and Station cards are left to set work (P16).
  • The session's best-of-three match and sideboarding between games are not part of this: a sideboard reaches the engine through the game table and the scenario builder only.
  • Review: a Warden for each milestone or half of one, a Mason before the foundations (the baseline audit), one after the module passes and one at the end, one Archivist, no Sage.

Models by name

tier:opus for design, foundations, keystones and every review. tier:sonnet for the rulings audits, the lint sweeps, partner and simple cards, measurements and the test conversions. Nothing is tier:fable: the designs in the plan and in each bead come from the one Fable session that planned this. A bead filed later follows the same policy.

Order

The whole epic waits for by-bajq to close.

  • Milestone 1 (baseline, guards and contracts), milestone 2 (the keystone audit) and milestone 3 (the card workflow) start together.
  • Each module pass of milestone 4 waits for the contracts it follows and its crate's lint sweep; the rules passes wait for the two kernel passes that settle the mutators.
  • The foundations gate (a Mason) follows milestone 4. Milestones 5, 6 and 7, the three keystone families, wait for it and then run in parallel.
  • The final gate (a Mason) follows everything. The Archivist is last.

Edges are on the beads that need a result.

Whole-run acceptance

  • Every keystone and partner in the plan's table is implemented, with every clause of its Oracle text, passes just card check, and is in the rulings ledger and the keystone pool. The 49 cards of by-bajq are in the ledger with every ruling a scenario or a reason.
  • clippy::wildcard_enum_match_arm and the four cast lints are denied in beryl_kernel, beryl_rules and beryl_card; State::in_candidate_order is not callable from the rules; every scenario passes in both layouts and under three slot numberings; rules functions take typed references.
  • Every file of the kernel and the rules declares what it implements and data/rule-ownership-allowlist.json is empty. just citations coverage runs.
  • A card with no scenarios module builds and passes every lane, and the plan's section E8 has the measured cost of twelve new cards against the 121 before.
  • just efficiency compile reports per-crate times, and three costs were reduced with before and after numbers.
  • The count gate was never raised for a mechanism. Each family's present-cost number is within 1.25 times or recorded with its cause. State<Small> is within 20,480 bytes.
  • just fuzz --pool keystones plays games containing each family with no invariant failure and no stall other than a budget.
  • The two plan documents are retired into the technical documents by the Archivist.

Code map

  • rust/crates/beryl_card/src/: the vocabulary. effect/, ability.rs, cost.rs, trigger.rs, filter.rs, continuous.rs, definition.rs (CardDef, Face), check.rs (what a definition is refused for), render/ (a definition back to Oracle text).
  • rust/crates/beryl_kernel/src/: the state. lib.rs is the contract every change amends. object.rs (ObjectRef), ids.rs, stack.rs, pending.rs, turn.rs, new.rs, zone.rs and mutate.rs (mutators), query.rs and copiable.rs, effects.rs, memos.rs, trackers.rs, table.rs (GameTable, Capability), layout.rs and relayout.rs (Small, Large), order.rs and bitboard.rs, invariants.rs.
  • rust/crates/beryl_rules/src/: the rules. lib.rs is its contract. engine.rs, turn.rs, start.rs, cast/ and casting.rs, resolve.rs (the replay transaction), choices.rs, interpret/, combat.rs and combat/assignment.rs, layers/, replace.rs, actions/, keywords/, sba.rs, reference.rs, tests/.
  • rust/crates/beryl_cards/src/: derive/ (the scenario generator), tests/mutation/ (the mutation check), library/ (hand-written scenarios of rules-library effects), decks.rs (KEYSTONE_FAMILIES). rust/crates/beryl_card_build/ writes the set crates' generated sources. rust/cards/<crate>/<set>/<card>.rs: one file a card.
  • rust/crates/beryl_scenario/ and beryl_scenario_types/: the runner and the builder.
  • tools/src/beryl_tools/: card.py and its siblings (just card), citations.py, checks.py and lanes.py (how a check is added), xmage.py, forge.py, card_facts.py (the oracle exporters), scale.py, nightly.py. efficiency/: the efficiency store.
  • Tests: just test-rust; just card check <name>; just oracle <names>; just fuzz; just exhaust; just count-gate; just citations; just check.
