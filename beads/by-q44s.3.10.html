Abilities on the stack are scene objects (ad39eb11a): placed, moved, drawn as Role::Ability cards

Description

Read first: docs/beryl-3d.md 'Objects and placement' and 'The frame' (Resolved, Role::Ability); the epic by-q44s ('Rules for every bead'); rust/beryl3d/AGENTS.md. Code: beryl_scene src/table/place.rs (stack, whose comment says abilities are drawn by the flat layer), src/table/scene.rs (SceneObject, Table::objects_for, Table::write), src/play/step.rs and compile.rs, src/frame.rs (the sizes OBJECTS, ELEMENTS, DRAWS), src/layout/mod.rs (FIRST_ID, 1 << 16). Protocol: rust/crates/beryl_protocol/src/script/ mod.rs ('Names': an ability is a number its emerge gives it, from 1 and never used again; a view's stack does not name its abilities), script/table.rs (Entry::id), script/mod.rs (Reset::abilities), protocol/view.rs (StackItem::Ability). Web: ui/src/stage/layout.ts (abilityName, StackAbility), ui/src/components/Stack.tsx (StackItem), ui/src/components/Card.tsx (AbilityPlate).

The decision. An ability on the stack is an object of the 3D table like a spell: emerge flies it out of its source, arrows start and end on it, and a prompt marks it. It is drawn as a card that shows its source's face; the plate the web cuts for it is milestone 3's drawn frame.

Build:

  • The table keeps the numbers of the abilities on the stack, bottom first: from Reset::abilities when a script resets, from Act::Emerge { id, .. } as it plays, and from the id of each Entry of the script's table when a script ends.
  • Placement gains abilities: Vec<(u32, Rest)>, sorted by number, and place::stack places every item of the view's stack, spells and abilities, bottom first, at layout.stack + layout.stack_step * index. Do not change where items stand: by-q44s.3.25 does. Table::placement takes the numbers as a slice.
  • Compiled gains ability_tracks: Vec<(u32, Track)>, which StepOut::segment fills for Subject::Ability. Places::pose, Places::rest and Show::reach answer for an ability. emerge and vanish stay instant changes here: by-q44s.3.11 builds their motion.
  • Table::write writes each ability: Role::Ability, named by StackItem::Ability::name, with ElementId((1 << 20) + number), Place::Card with the stack's zone and its controller's side, and the board-size face of its source's name, or the blank layer 0 when the source is unknown. A test asserts that FIRST_ID plus frame::LAYER_ELEMENTS is below 1 << 20.
  • Add frame::ABILITIES: usize = 64 to the sizes of ELEMENTS and DRAWS.
  • Change the comment of stack and add two sentences to the doc's 'Objects and placement'.

Tests, in beryl_scene/tests/table.rs and tests/play.rs: a view whose stack is a spell and two abilities, reset with the numbers 3 and 4, prints ability lines at the second and third places of the stack; a script that emerges ability 5 ends with it there, and one that vanishes it ends without it; rest_equals_placement and the duplicates check find nothing; a frame after the script allocates nothing.

Run: just b3d cargo-test -p beryl_scene; just b3d list | head; just b3d gate; just b3d lane.

  • table/stack.rs (new): OnStack {Spell, Ability{id,side}, Unnumbered} and place_stack, which places every stack item bottom first at layout.stack + stack_step*index. Table::placement(view, abilities) and place(view, abilities, ..) take the numbers (tests pass &[] when the view has no abilities). Table::abilities() and ability_rest(id). write_abilities/ability_at/write_ability draw each as Role::Ability, ElementId(ABILITY_IDS + id % ABILITY_IDS), ABILITY_IDS = 1<<20, face = board layer of StackItem::Ability::name when source is Some. Motion gains ability_tracks.
