Beryl 3D sweep: report quick steps before the VM counts

Close reason

Sweep runs quick steps first, saves the build and files their failures, then gpu/frame-counts under their own lock; per-part seconds logged

Description

Symptom. The Beryl 3D sweep (just b3d sweep, tools/src/beryl_tools/beryl3d/sweep.py) is the only place that runs Beryl 3D's tests after a main-workspace change, and the only place that runs a fast-check step the fast check cut off at its hard limit. On 2026-11-15 its nine runs took 496, 933, 799, 307, 1360, 1400, 2146, 4837 and 7540 seconds, and the run that started at 16:35Z was still inside b3d gate --counts (Callgrind in the beryl3d-bench VM, built with -j 1) after 1 h 50 min. A sweep holds a lock, so every later sweep is skipped with "another sweep is in progress" (by-q44s.1.51 could not verify its own step for that reason). Failures therefore arrive hours late: the cargo-test and clippy breakage from 7daf5c1ce (by-q44s.1.25, a main-workspace change) was filed 1.5 h after it landed, and today 15 of 60 fast-check runs of the beryl3d lane's cargo-test and 11 of 32 runs of its gate step were cut off at the 180 s hard limit (runs.jsonl, timeout: true), so those pushes landed with Beryl 3D's tests unrun until the next sweep.

A second cost. The sweep saves the workspace's build directory to $BERYL_CACHE/b3d/target (save_target, called at the end of sweep_run) only after every step has finished. At 18:36Z the saved directory was from 14:58Z, 3.5 hours old. Every new worktree clones it, so the first just b3d command or fast check in a worktree recompiles the engine and all card crates that changed since (beryl_session, beryl_cli and beryl_cards are path dependencies of beryl_3d, beryl_assets, beryl_record and beryl_scene's tests). Measured in a fresh worktree at load 27 to 50: the first just b3d tree took 43 s and the first just b3d png 47 s, the beryl3d lane cold took 132 CPU-s (181 s on the clock; 51 crates compiled, all card crates among them), and after a one-line beryl_scene edit in the same worktree only 33 CPU-s (74 s on the clock, nothing from the main workspace recompiled).

Proposed change.

  1. In sweep_run, run the lane's steps and smoke first, save the build directory as soon as clippy and cargo-test have passed, and only then run gpu and frame-counts. Bisect and file the quick steps' failures before the slow steps start.
  2. Give the slow steps (gpu, frame-counts) a lock of their own, so a quick sweep can start while a counts run is still going, or run them at most every few hours. Their bisection keeps its own time limit.
  3. Report in log.jsonl the seconds of each part, so the next audit can see the trend.

Expected gain. A main-workspace change that breaks Beryl 3D, or a deferred cargo-test failure, is filed within about 15 minutes of a slow-CI pass instead of 1.5 to 3 hours. The cached build directory is at most one quick sweep old, which brings a fresh worktree's first beryl3d lane run closer to the 33 CPU-s warm figure and makes fewer fast-check steps hit the 180 s limit. by-wzq9 (the lane over its budget) is the other half of this problem.

Acceptance criteria

A sweep saves $BERYL_CACHE/b3d/target and files any failure of the lane steps and smoke before gpu or frame-counts start; a quick sweep can run while frame-counts runs; log.jsonl records each part's seconds; tests in tools/tests cover the order and the lock; the time of a quick sweep at the staging tip is in a comment, with the load.

Quick sweep timed at f07a94c57 (staging tip bda009c44 plus this change): by b3d sweep-run --part quick --dry-run, run directly in a fresh worktree, because a real sweep on the old tools held .lock. It took 478 s on the clock (832 user-s, 246 sys-s), with the load average going from 13.8 to 30.0. It started from the 3.5-hour-old cached build directory, so the build was cold: clippy 321 s, cargo-test 317 s, gate 205 s, smoke 361 s, all passed. Decisions: (1) The slow part (gpu, frame-counts) runs only after a quick part that passed, because with a broken build its failures would only repeat the quick part's beads. (2) Each part runs in its own tree, quick in sweep/work and slow in sweep/work-slow, because coverage.in_tree holds its folder's lock for the whole run and names the tree after the commit. (3) There is no 'at most every few hours' limit: the slow lock alone lets quick sweeps run freely, and a firing whose slow lock is held records parts.slow.skipped. (4) Each part bisects from the last sweep in which that part passed, and log records from before this change count as passing both parts when their outcome is ok.

Derek Thurn2026-11-15

Handoff: tools/src/beryl_tools/beryl3d/sweep.py. SweepStep.part is QUICK or SLOW. Request.parts() and run_sweep loop over the parts under lock(part) (.lock for quick, which start_background also tests, and .slow.lock for slow). The new sweep-run --part flag selects a part's steps, and just b3d sweep --part runs one part without logging. last_passed(records, part) reads the record's parts. Log records gain parts: {quick: {seconds, code}, slow: {seconds, code} | {skipped}}. A part's output goes to last-<part>-output.txt. Gotcha: the firing passes --part to the swept commit's sweep-run, so sweeping a commit older than this change with --commit fails until the tools there know --part. Tests are in tools/tests/test_beryl3d_sweep.py (part order, per-part bisect base, quick part running while a slow lock is held).
