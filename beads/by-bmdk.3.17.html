Close reason

Landed searchable manual pile viewers, library look and shuffle, and sideboard drag on staging. Live browser and two-seat redaction checks passed.

Description

Read first: epic by-bmdk, sections 'Rules for every bead', 'Contracts' and 'Code map'. Design: docs/manual-multiplayer.md ("the doc"), section 'Viewing piles', and the appendix tables 'The library' (the viewer rows) and 'Graveyard, exile and sideboard'.

How interface beads in this milestone fit together: ui/src/table/client.ts (bead "Table screen") is the manual table's pointer client. It owns the selection, sends actions with tableAct from ui/src/ipc.ts, and lets each interaction register handlers from its own file. Put your code in the file this bead names and register it; change client.ts only to add a registration point it lacks. Check your work on a live manual table in a headless browser: ui/scripts/table.mjs (same bead) starts one through the HTTP server and gives a Playwright page. Add one scripted check per behavior to ui/scripts/kept-table-<topic>.mjs and run it with just ui-test <title>.

File: ui/src/table/viewers.ts and TableViewer.tsx, built on ZoneViewer in ui/src/game/Piles.tsx and the lists in ui/src/game/zoneViews.ts. Extend ZoneViewer with optional props; an engine game passes none and looks the same.

  • A viewer shows a pile's cards in a grid: a graveyard, exile, a sideboard, all or part of a library (after look), or cards revealed to the viewer.
  • A search field filters by name; "Group by type" and sort by name or mana value.
  • Cards in a viewer take the same clicks, drags and menus as cards anywhere: give them stage names the TableClient classifies as viewer_card, or route their events to the client; choose by reading how ZoneViewer cards are drawn (borrowed table cards or their own elements) and say which on the bead.
  • Opening a library view sends look (whole, top n or bottom n); the other seat sees the cards lifted. Closing a view of the whole library offers "Shuffle", highlighted, with "Don't shuffle" beside it.
  • F3 views the library, F4 the graveyard, Cmd+F3 the sideboard. Escape closes the most recent view. "Hide" (Option+H) closes the viewer of cards revealed to the viewer.
  • A drop onto a viewer moves the card to that zone; a drag from the sideboard viewer moves a card out of the sideboard.

Checks (kept-table-viewers.mjs): open a graveyard, search, play a card from it; look at the top 3 and drag one to the bottom; close a full library view and shuffle; sideboard drag. Engine checks for pile viewers still pass (just ui-test for two of them). A fixture or kept moment with a library viewer open for the generic audits.

Acceptance criteria

Viewers search, group and sort; their cards take clicks, drags and menus; library views send look and offer Shuffle on close; keys work; engine viewers are unchanged; a viewer state is under the generic audits.

The adapter provides TableAction { close_look: {} } for Escape and Don't shuffle when closing a library viewer opened with look. Send it through TableClient alongside the local close. It lowers the lifted cards for both seats and keeps learned identities. The existing shuffle action also lowers the lift. A card move through the pile or a passed turn can end the lift first.

The sideboard has no card pose while its viewer is closed. In the stage probe for by-bmdk.3.7, attaching a card directly from the sideboard produced a 129 px jump in the first 33 ms of a Fly step. The shipped table-drawn fixture checks that the sideboard is absent at rest and attaches cards from hand. When testing moves from a sideboard viewer, check the Fly origin as well as the final placement.
