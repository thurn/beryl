Description

Why

The count gate (rust/crates/beryl_bench/count-gate.toml) proves a mechanism costs the WU mirror nothing. It cannot say what a rollout costs with Blood Moon on the battlefield, and no deck contains these cards. Decision R3 sets that bar.

Why the measurement is paired by step

The obvious measurement is wrong: roll out positions with the family in play, roll out the same positions without it, and compare instructions per decision. The two rollouts play different games from their first decision. Glorious Anthem makes a game with more combat and more creatures dying, which are expensive decisions; Trinisphere makes one in which a player often can only pass, which is the cheapest decision there is. Either moves instructions per decision by more than the mechanism does, so that ratio can fail with no cost in the mechanism and pass with a real one.

So the control is never rolled out. It is rebuilt at every step.

What to build

Plan: Designs 1, "The present-cost corpus". Read docs/benchmarking.md first.

  1. A family is a name, a list of cards and how many of each replace which cards of decks::WU_CONTROL (still 60 cards). Keep the families as data beside the benchmark.
  2. Cutting positions. A command plays seeded games of the greedy agent between two such decks and keeps mid-game positions in which a card of the family is on the battlefield or has an effect in force, as the existing corpus was cut. Keep some from the turn a family's card arrives, so the cost of its entering is in the sample. Pin them with a test the way the WU corpus is pinned.
  3. The control state. A function takes a state and a family and returns a copy with the family taken out: through the board handle (State::board), each of the family's permanents becomes a prop of the same card types that does nothing, and every entry of the Effects store whose source is a card of the family is ended. Nothing else changes. Add props to beryl_card_shared if none fits. A family's card in a hand, library or graveyard stays: it costs nothing there unless one of its abilities works in that zone, and then it is swapped too.
  4. The paired step. The benchmark rolls out each family position under the rollout policy. At every decision it has the state S and the policy's choice c. It builds the control S' from S, and counts the instructions of apply(c) and the next that follows on S and on S'. The rollout continues from S; S' is dropped. Two sums accumulate over every step of every rollout. If c is not a candidate in S', by what it does and not by its index (match it as a save matches an answer, beryl_session::save), the step is left out of both sums and counted as skipped. Building S' is outside what is counted: use Callgrind's toggles or client requests so only the two transitions are collected, and say in docs/benchmarking.md how.
  5. The gate. just count-gate --family <name> reports both sums, their ratio, the steps paired and the steps skipped, and fails over the ratio in count-gate.toml ([family.<name>] ratio = 1.25). It also fails if more than a tenth of the steps were skipped, since the ratio then says little. It runs in slow CI's counts lane for families that have a corpus.
  6. Prove the harness on a family that exists today: Swift Reconfiguration and Leonin Scimitar, the two cards whose static abilities the layers apply. As a check on the method, a family of one do-nothing prop must come out at 1.00.

What the paired step does not see: work the mechanism did earlier that changed the state (a card exiled where it would have gone to a graveyard). That is history both sides share, and its cost was counted at the step that did it.

Each later group has a bead that adds its family and records its number.

Bars (every bead of this epic)

  • The absent-cost gate holds with no bump (just count-gate; R2 in the plan). The count is exact, so report the count before and after in the close reason. If it cannot hold after three attempts, stop and say so on the epic: this is the one thing that stops the run.
  • size_of::<State<Small>>() stays within 20,480 bytes. Report the size if you changed it.
  • The frozen saves replay and rollouts_never_change holds (just test-rust). A change to what the WU mirror's rollouts do is a bug in this epic.
  • Rules code cites the Comprehensive Rules with a quotation (root AGENTS.md); just citations.
  • A new card follows the card-writing skill: just card new, just card check <name> (Beryl, XMage and Forge), every instruction carried out by its own scenarios, and just specs <card> passing the generic UI checks from both seats. A disagreement with an oracle is settled by the rules and recorded in data/oracle-divergences.json.
  • Read the card's rulings before writing its scenarios (just card brief <name> prints Scryfall's). Each statement in a ruling that a scenario can show is a scenario of the card. Where one cannot be shown yet, the close reason names the ruling and why.
  • A rules bug found on the way gets its docs/bugs.md entry.
  • If the design in the plan is wrong for the code, build what is right and amend the plan's section in the same push. Never wait for an answer: record the choice in this bead.
