Close reason

Per-step placement is incremental and clone-free: stress-1024 worst frame 111.6M -> 26.8M (4M target not met; rest filed as by-q44s.19)

Description

Symptom: just b3d gate --counts measures the worst frame of stress-1024 at 111,561,447 instructions and of stress-255 at 28,304,611, against the targets of 4,000,000 and 2,000,000 in docs/beryl-3d.md ('What it is held to'). Both are frame 30, where Client::handle compiles the second view's script of 40 flights. The cost is now linear in the number of permanents (the quadratic land sort and piles were fixed in by-q44s.1.49), but large per step.

Why (callgrind self cost of that frame, in the beryl3d-bench machine under ~/gungraun/b3d-gate/beryl_inspect/frame_counts/frames/ranked.stress_1024_0): about 55% is malloc and free, plus ObjectView::clone and its drop. Motion::step in rust/beryl3d/crates/beryl_scene/src/play/compile.rs runs, for each of the 40 steps, write (which clears view.objects and clones every object out of Played, each with a String name and several Vecs) and then Table::placement (rust/beryl3d/crates/beryl_scene/src/table/place.rs place), which places all 1,024 objects again, and then compares every slot's old and new rest. So a script of k steps costs about k x n object clones and placements.

Possible fixes, cheapest first: (1) let place read objects by reference from Played (a small trait or a struct of slices) so write no longer clones every object per step; (2) re-place only the rows and piles a step's act touched, keeping the other objects' rests, since a fly from or to the battlefield changes at most its source and destination rows; (3) compare only the slots whose rows changed. Keep every segment exactly as now: just b3d check, just b3d cargo-test and the scripts' recorded values must not change.

Then run just b3d gate --counts --update (about 30 minutes in beryl3d-bench) and commit the lowered limits in data/beryl3d/gates.json with the old and new counts in the commit body.

Acceptance criteria

The worst frame of stress-255 is under 2,000,000 instructions and of stress-1024 under 4,000,000 in just b3d gate --counts, or the bead records what stops it with the measured numbers; gates.json holds the lowered limits; segments are unchanged (just b3d cargo-test, just b3d gate and just b3d check on the recordings pass).

Target not reached; measured in beryl3d-bench: stress-1024 worst frame 111,561,447 -> 26,838,750 (target 4,000,000), stress-255 28,304,611 -> 6,713,316 (target 2,000,000). Handle allocations 135,385 -> 18,129 and 36,569 -> 5,580. All three suggested fixes are done: placement borrows from the played table, and each step re-places and compares only the groups it touched. What remains is spread out: each step still re-lays a whole ~256-card row (name sorts, one Vec per pile group), plus malloc/free (~25%), Played::leave's retain over the battlefield, two whole-table placements per compile, and FrameBuilder::finish. Filed by-q44s.19 for those. Deviation from the bead: I added Table::apply_noting to beryl_protocol (outside rust/beryl3d), so the push also ran the main lanes.

D
Derek Thurn2026-11-15

Handoff: table/place.rs now splits placement into 15 groups (Group: each pile, hand and battlefield row, plus the stack) over a borrowed Zones struct (Zones::of_view / Zones::of_table). place_group places one group. In play/compile.rs, Motion keeps per-group members and per-slot groups. Motion::place applies the act with Played::apply_noting (new in beryl_protocol script/table.rs; it reports replaced or removed slots and library reorders), marks the touched slots' old and new groups plus the stack as dirty, and places only those into fresh. step compares only Motion::affected slots, and commit merges the result. The first step, and the step after a failed apply, place every group, because the view before lists hands in view order, not slot order. With records kept, a debug build asserts after every step that the incremental result equals a full placement (no assert in the gate's no-records path, so its allocation counts stay a running game's). Reviewers: check Group::of agrees with members() for every zone.
