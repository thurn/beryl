Table space, camera and placement at rest on staging at 6a827096e

Description

Read first: docs/beryl-3d.md 'The table' ('Space and camera', 'Objects and placement'). For behavior, read ui/src/game/battlefield.ts, tableRows.ts, handLayout.ts, stackLayout.ts, zoneCards.ts and perspective.ts: they say which rows exist, how a row overlaps and piles identical permanents, and where each zone stands. Reproduce the behavior in real coordinates; do not port the 1600 by 900 numbers.

In rust/beryl3d/crates/beryl_scene, src/table:

  • World units are millimetres, the table is the plane z = 0, a card is 63 by 88.
  • Camera (position, pitch, vertical field of view) for the desktop layout, as data. Use a first estimate that frames both players' rows; milestone 2 fits it to Arena.
  • The fit: the camera's distance along its axis is chosen so the layout's required table rectangle fits the window inside the safe area.
  • One scene object per game object, keyed by ObjectView's public slot, kept across updates.
  • Placement functions, each pure in (view, window): battlefield rows for both players (full size, then overlapped, then piles of identical permanents with a count), each hand, the stack, library, graveyard and exile piles with 0.3 of height per card. An overlapped card lies 0.3 above the one it covers and keeps at least 24 by 24 logical points uncovered. A row that still cannot give every card that much shows as many as fit, from a scroll offset held in the client, and does not draw the rest; the drag and wheel that change the offset are milestone 2.
  • On an update with a reset script, every object is put at its placement. frame emits a Resolved (role Card, the card's name, projected corners, depth, state bits for tapped and face down) and a Draw per visible card, and one Draw for the table.

frame writes through the contract's FrameBuilder. Also add a module testing, behind the driver feature, with builders for a PlayerView holding a few named cards in each zone and for a script of steps. This bead's tests use it, and so do other beads'.

Tests: placement is the same for the same view and window; a 255-permanent battlefield keeps every drawn card inside the table rectangle with its strip uncovered, and draws none of the cards scrolled out of view; projected corners of a card at the table's centre land where a hand calculation says; resizing from 1440x900 to 2560x1080 keeps the required rectangle in view.

Run: just b3d cargo-test -p beryl_scene.

What the prototype found (rust/prototypes/beryl3d, 2026-11-14; read its code for a working example, then build it properly):

  • A first camera fitted by eye to Arena: pitch 24 degrees from straight down, vertical field of view 30 degrees, looking at (0, -30, 0), with a required rectangle of x in -470..470 and y in -290..215 millimetres, which includes the strip the viewer's hand covers. glam 0.34 deprecates Mat4::perspective_rh and look_at_rh; use glam::camera::rh::proj::directx::perspective and glam::camera::rh::view::look_at_mat4 (Z in 0..1, as wgpu wants). See core/camera.rs.
  • Every card in a row must lie 0.3 above the one before it, overlapping or not: a tapped card is 88 wide, wider than an untapped row's step, and at equal heights the two flicker where they overlap (the doc now says so).
  • Hands placed relative to the camera work as the doc says: a screen_pose(ndc, depth, rotation, height) that turns a screen point, a depth in front of the eye and a card height in screen fractions into a world pose (core/camera.rs, core/place.rs).
  • Exile at the doc's earlier place beside the graveyard fell off the right edge for the opponent; the prototype puts it above the graveyard.

Acceptance criteria

A hand-built view yields a frame whose cards are placed in every zone, from both seats; the tests above pass; no placement function reads anything but the view and the window.

Decisions: (1) Rows follow the doc's order — full size, then overlap to the least strip, then pile twins only when overlap alone fails, then scroll — not the web UI's always-pile twins and 7-land rule. (2) The lands row takes lands that are not creatures; the UI's mana-rock rule needs card text, which the client core does not have yet, so mana artifacts stand in the others row. (3) Opponent rows mirror only y (lands stay at the viewer's left, as the web UI's ROWS); piles turn half a turn (opponent library top right). (4) Rows' right edge 320 and stack at x=370 so the stack clears the opponent's exile pile. (5) Every update snaps objects to rest (no verbs yet); the verbs bead replaces that for non-reset scripts. (6) Manual-table orders (view.table.orders) are not read yet for hands and exile. (7) Card face layers are 0 until the faces bead assigns them.
