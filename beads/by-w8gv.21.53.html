Findings, each with its frame (films: just motion --film point; folders under ~/Library/Caches/beryl/motion/<checkout>/film/point):

  1. Attack and block lines, phone, both seats (phone/theirs/combat-15-block-line table +267; phone/yours +80 and +280). Three thin gold lines meet the blocker: its block line, and two attack lines that run under it on their way to the life total, one entering its bottom centre where the block line leaves. Only the block line may meet a blocker. Either bend an attack line round any card in its way (lines.ts straight knows only its two ends: the stage would have to give a line what stands between, which is a design change, since a shape is a function of its two ends today), or draw the attack line otherwise on a phone, where attackers wear no chevron. Dropping the line to the player is the user's call (item 2 of the human question below), not this bead's.
  2. Stack arrow, phone, both seats (phone/yours/zones-19 ticks +97 to +164; phone/theirs strip +110 on). Its head lands on the seam between the two items and the arc hooks back at its own item's edge. A phone's stack items lie otherwise than the desktop's (phone.ts): they do not overlap as lines.ts overlap() expects, so loop() is not the shape drawn there. Carry the arc across so its head lies on the target item's name strip, about a third of the strip in from the seam.
  3. Note: a phone's target arrow is dead straight when the stack and its target line up (phone/yours zones-46 table +135): give it a slight bow, as the desktop's has, so that it is not read as a line of combat (lines.ts onto: its point in front leans by ONTO.lean of the way back, which is nothing when the two line up).
  4. Note: on a phone the source line runs over the face of a creature between the stack and its source (phone/yours/cast-11 strip +277 to +343).
