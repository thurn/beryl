Description

Design every phone moment DESIGN.md leaves undesigned, so the implementation beads can build them without further design decisions. This bead produces a design, not code. Read the run epic, then /Users/dthurn/beryl-mobile-prototype/DESIGN.md in full, look at its screenshots/, and serve the prototype (cd /Users/dthurn/beryl-mobile-prototype/prototype && python3 -m http.server 8000) to feel its layout at 393×759 (Playwright MCP or the built-in browser at a phone viewport).

Scope

DESIGN.md's "Not covered yet" list, each as a portrait design that follows its Principles (cards are the interface, no chrome the cards carry, touch-down replaces hover, keep the desktop's visual language, halve the words, open choices directly with a way to decline, no priority the player would only pass):

  1. Blocking, damage assignment order/division, combat arrows (attack and block lines in portrait).
  2. Targeting after casting, arrows from the stack to targets.
  3. Mana payment and the mana picker; activated abilities and the ability chooser.
  4. Choices: scry, search (with "find nothing"), look at, order triggers, modes, X costs, and any other DecisionView kinds the engine emits (check ui/src/generated/protocol.ts and ui/src/game/decisions.ts, choices.ts, ChoiceOverlay.tsx, ModeChooser.tsx, AbilityChooser.tsx, CastStep.tsx, ManaFlow.tsx to enumerate them; every one needs a phone design).
  5. Mulligan, game start (start screen: agent choice, new game, resume), game over.
  6. The game log, settings (sound, stops), the bug report button and its flow on a phone (freeze, region select by touch drag, describe with the on-screen keyboard, Save, Share), undo.
  7. Damage marks, +1/+1 and other counters, attached auras and equipment, exile links, drawn on the portrait board.
  8. Boards past 3 creature rows, more than ~10 other permanents a side.
  9. Animations and the opponent's activity: how the desktop's narration, beats and CardLayer motion (Narration.tsx, beats.ts, playback.ts, TurnBanner.tsx, Risen.tsx) carry to portrait.
  10. Very small phones (<375) and tablets/landscape: state what happens (e.g. scale the 375 layout; landscape locked) rather than designing them fully.

For each: which desktop component it reuses and how it is reshaped, layout in px against DESIGN.md's height budget at 393×759 and 375×647, gestures, the words on screen (≤ half the desktop's), how it declines, and the fixture/board that would show it in just ui-shots (name the scripted board in rust/crates/beryl_cli/src/boards.rs to reuse or the one to add). Mobile-specific values join DESIGN.md's table; add no new colors or tokens.

Design

Phone design: the undesigned moments

This extends /Users/dthurn/beryl-mobile-prototype/DESIGN.md ("DESIGN"). Its Principles, Constraints, Layout, Components and Gestures stand. Nothing here adds a color, token or font. Everything reuses desktop components and the logic modules (decisions.ts, choices.ts, casting.ts, combat.ts, tablePick.ts, untapChoice.ts, log.ts, stops.ts). Phone code under ui/src/mobile/ only lays out and dispatches.

Who owns what

  • §0 (shared pieces): whichever bead builds a piece first builds it for all. Most likely the "choices and game flow" bead builds the tray and the "combat, targeting and mana" bead builds the prompt row.
  • §1–3: by-7pm.3.2, Phone combat, targeting and mana.
  • §4–5: by-7pm.3.3, Phone choices and game flow.
  • §6 (log, settings, undo), §7–10: by-7pm.3.4, Phone board marks, activity and menus.
  • §6 (bug report): by-7pm.3.6, Phone bug report UI.

Shots go in ui/shots.json as phone-<moment> at 393×759 and phone-<moment>-se at 375×647, on route ?phone&fixture=<fixture>. "Fixture" below means an entry in ui/fixtures.json. "New board" means a Scenario to add in rust/crates/beryl_cli/src/boards.rs, plus its fixture.

0. Shared pieces

Every non-priority decision is built from four pieces, which mirror the desktop's surfaces:

  • the dock, which stays as it is;
  • the prompt row, the phone's FloatPrompt / Dock title and extra buttons;
  • the tray, the phone's ChoiceOverlay and ModeChooser;
  • the risen card, the phone's Risen.

The routing is the desktop's: choiceOf()/floats() and isOpening() / isCombat() decide which surface a decision gets. Phone code never re-derives what a decision asks.

0.1 Dock (unchanged position)
  • It holds the decision's primary answer only. That is the desktop's first button: Choice.confirm, answers[0], Declare/Finish's turn button, or CastStep's Cast.
  • The button is .button.button-primary, 40px tall, 18px type, min width 84. The label is text-fit down to 15px.
  • If a decision has no primary (it is answered by tapping a card or orb, e.g. target, triggerOrder, a small chooseObject), the dock is empty. Never show a disabled button.
  • The pass-on button shows only at priority, as DESIGN says.
  • The touch-down tooltip carries what the desktop's caption said, e.g. "3 attacking", "To End Step", the spell's name.
0.2 Prompt row (new; any non-priority decision that has a title or secondary buttons)
  • Position:
    • a 48px row directly under your strip: 672–720 at 393, 564–612 at 375;
    • while it shows, the hand fan translates down 54px (48 + 6 so rotated corners clear it), leaving 33px (393) or 29px (375) of each hand card plus the bottom inset;
    • nothing reflows, and the board keeps its size;
    • the hand slides with the CardLayer glide, or at once under reduced motion.
  • Contents, left to right, vertically centered, 6px gaps, 8px gutters:
    1. eye icon button (40×40, .icon-button), only while a tray is open. See Peek in 0.3.
    2. Title: 16px Times New Roman (the desktop .prompt-title face), one line, ellipsis, flexible width. Touch-down on it shows the zoom of the decision's source card, if any (touch-down replaces hover). Titles are in §1–5 and the coverage table.
    3. Secondary buttons: .button.button-secondary, 40px tall, 18px type text-fit to 15px, min width 64, right-aligned. At most 2. Cancel is always the rightmost.
  • Steppers (X, how many targets) sit in the title's place:
    • − and + icon buttons, 40×40;
    • between them, X = n in an 18px output (reuse .cast-x / .cast-x-value from CastStep.tsx);
    • the ends are disabled per xValues().
  • Cancel:
    • shows when decision.cancellable, or while the client-side chooser is open (cancelCast);
    • label "Cancel";
    • the system back gesture on Android also cancels.

The tray is for cards off the table, or cards picked from the hand: Arrange, Stack and Pick choices, Asks with cards, trigger order and the ability/cast chooser.

  • Short tray: it covers from the middle band's top to your strip's top, so your half is covered and the opponent's half stays lit and readable.

      • 4 cards: 90×126 (393) and 86×120 (375).

  • Peek: holding the prompt row's eye fades the tray to 0 over 120ms while the finger is down; release restores it. This is the desktop's "View Battlefield" / "Back to …", as touch-down.
  • What the tray covers:
    • the middle band, so the phase icon, the stack and undo, while it is open;
    • the resolving item has already left the stack (DecisionView.resolving).

    • no scrim.
  • Touch-down zooms it. The title can then stay short.
0.5 Glows (desktop classes, unchanged meanings)

1. Combat: attackers, blockers, damage, lines

Reuses:

  • combat.ts (Draft, declared, attackPlan, blockPlan, blockable, incoming);
  • Board's Declare/Finish logic, extracted where it is tied to Board;
  • Card.tsx combat markers;
  • components/Stack.tsx's Arrows, which anchors by cardId / lifeId, so phone cards keep those ids.

Declare attackers (attacker):

  • Eligible creatures glow cyan. A tap toggles one, and a stack adds one copy per tap, then clears (DESIGN).
  • An attacking creature slides 12px toward the middle. The creature zone's +24px slack holds it, so nothing reflows. It wears .combat-marker.attack-chevron over its top edge, scaled by the card's font size.
  • Planeswalker defenders (the desktop's rule):
    • the creature just tapped is selected (gold), and the defender's planeswalkers glow cyan;
    • tapping one aims it there, drawn as a red arrow-red from the attacker's top center to the planeswalker's bottom center;
    • tapping the opponent's orb or another creature leaves it on the player.
    • An attack on the player draws no line. The chevron and the badge carry it.
  • Incoming badge:
    • .life-incoming / .life-incoming-lethal ("−7"), 13px, at the left edge of the defending player's orb;
    • for the opponent, that is just left of their orb in the 48px strip;
    • for you, it is in your strip's free zone;
    • it shows only in the declare steps, as on desktop.
  • Dock: "No Attacks" → "Attack". Tooltip "N attacking".
  • Prompt row, no title:
    • "All" (the desktop's "Attack All") while ≥2 eligible creatures are not attacking;
    • "Clear" once anything is drafted.
  • Declining is "No Attacks", the dock with an empty draft.

Attack target (attackTarget):

  • The attacker glows gold. The planeswalkers and the opponent's orb glow cyan.
  • Dock: "Attack", defaulting to the player.
  • Title: "Attack where".

Declare blockers (blocker):

  • Your eligible blockers glow cyan.
  • Tap one: it turns gold, and the attackers blockable() allows glow cyan. Tap one of those attackers to assign the block. The blocker gets .combat-marker.block-arc.
  • A gold arrow-gold runs from the blocker's top center to the attacker's bottom center. It crosses the middle band and routes around the phase icon (AVOID).
  • For several blockers on one attacker, repeat; each draws its own line.
  • Tap an assigned blocker to clear it and select it again. Tap empty table to deselect.
  • The opponent's attackers have already slid 12px toward the middle with their chevrons. The incoming badge is in your strip.
  • Cards are never moved to align blockers with attackers. A stable layout beats short lines, because every line keeps its endpoints readable.
  • Dock: "No Blocks" → "Block". Tooltip "N blocking".
  • Prompt row: "Clear" once anything is drafted.
  • Block target (blockTarget): the blocker is gold and the attackers are cyan. Dock "Block". Title "Block which".

Damage division (damageDivision, with lethal candidates):

  • The attacker glows gold and its blockers glow cyan.
  • Tap blockers in the order to assign lethal damage. Each tapped blocker gets a 12px .mark-badge with its order number (1, 2…) at the top left, and its plaque shows the damage it will take (§7's .card-pt with stat-down).
  • Dock: "Assign", the done candidate. Title: "Assign damage".
  • The desktop says "Assign Damage", "Choose blockers to destroy" and "Assign combat damage" (7 words). The phone says 3.

Combat damage at priority: passTo.combatDamage labels the dock "Damage" (desktop "Resolve Damage" / "Combat damage").

2. Targeting and arrows from the stack

Reuses decisions.ts (prompt, upToOne, interactions), Board's target picking (extract it), Arrows, and PromptCard faces.

  • On cast: the spell moves to the stack (CR 601.2a), so the middle band grows to 110 and the spell sits there in gold. Legal targets glow cyan. Players glow on their orbs (.life-aimed).
  • Tap a target to answer. Each target picked draws a cyan arrow-cyan from the stack card's edge facing it (top edge for the opponent's side, bottom for yours) to the target's nearest edge center, or to the orb.
  • Off-table targets (graveyard, exile) open a short tray with those cards as a Pick. A tap answers, and there is no confirm.
  • Up to one (targetCount {0,1} flattened by upToOne): pick on the board, with prompt row "None" (desktop "No Target").
  • General targetCount: a stepper in the prompt row. Dock "Choose". Title "Targets".
  • Title: "Target" (desktop "Choose a target for …"). Touch-down on the title shows the spell.
  • Decline: prompt row "Cancel" while cancellable.
  • Arrows at priority:
    • only the top item's targets are drawn persistently;
    • a lower item's targets, and an ability's gold arrow from its source, show while that stack card is touched (touch-down replaces the desktop's hover on sourced);
    • targeted cards on the top item glow cyan, as on desktop;
    • the zoom of a targeted card adds the note "Targeted by …" (cyan edge, DESIGN).
  • Ids: stack cards keep itemId; lower items keep peekId. The middle band's stack cards are 68px wide, 44px apart, so a lower item is always partly visible to touch.

3. Mana, the mana picker, abilities and the chooser

Auto-pay stays the default (casting.ts firstChoice, castPlan), so most casts never show payment.

Cast step (asksCastStep: several casts, or an X):

  • Releasing a lifted hand card above the hand (DESIGN) opens the chooser tray (below) when there are several casts, or the X stepper when there is only an X.
  • After a cast with an X is picked, the prompt row shows the X stepper.
  • Dock: "Cast". Prompt row: "Cancel".
  • Full control with several payment plans: the lands the current plan taps glow gold (paying), and sources only another plan uses glow green. Tapping one switches plans, as on desktop.

paymentPlan decision:

  • Plan by tapping lands, as above.
  • Dock: "Pay".
  • Prompt row: "Tap Nothing" when it is offered.
  • Title: "Pay".

  • The glowing sources are tapped to pay.
  • Title: "Pay" plus the cost in ManaSymbols (the CostText component).
  • Prompt row: "Cancel" while cancellable.

unlessPays: a risen card (the source). Dock "Pay {cost}" with symbols. Prompt row "Don't Pay". Title: none, because the risen card and the cost say it.

Mana picker:

  • Tapping a land with only mana abilities at priority taps it. One color adds at once.
  • Several colors open the desktop .mana-picker: a row of 40×40 .mana-picker-item ManaSymbol buttons in a pill, centered over the land and 8px above it, clamped to the 8px gutters, over .menu-scrim. A scrim tap closes it.

  • The desktop ManaPool, 18px symbols, in your strip's free zone (70–146 at 393, 70–137 at 375). Past 3 symbols it shows "+n".

10. Small phones, tablets, landscape

  • Under 375 CSS px wide (360-wide Androids, 320 iPhone SE 1st gen):
    • lay out at 375 wide and scale the game screen by width / 375 with a CSS transform on the root;
    • layout height is innerHeight × 375 / width; 360×640 becomes 375×667;
    • text scales with it: 11.5px at 360, which is accepted as the one exception to the 12px rule, and touch targets stay ≥ 38px;
    • the start screen and sheets reflow instead of scaling.
  • Heights under 647 at a usable width: the budget's clamps (L ≥ 36, O ≥ 48) and the creature floor (§8) absorb it. Below 600 the hand strip drops to 50% of a hand card.
  • Landscape: locked to portrait on phones (iOS UISupportedInterfaceOrientations portrait only; Android screenOrientation="portrait"). No landscape design.
  • Tablets:
    • iOS ships iPhone-only (TARGETED_DEVICE_FAMILY = 1), so iPads run it in iPhone compatibility mode, portrait and letterboxed.
    • Android tablets get the portrait phone layout centered at 430 wide, with the tabletop background filling the sides.
    • Phone mode is the only mobile layout. The desktop layout is not offered on touch devices in V1.
  • Wider than 430 (large Android phones at 412–480): layout at min(width, 430), centered, with the tabletop filling the sides.
