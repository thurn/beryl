Description

Part of Milestone 1 (epic by-b0q). Read by-b0q.3.5 (the expert and its 21-round review loop) and the Warden findings on by-b0q.3.11 first.

The expert met decision 6's bar (5 seeded expert-v-expert games, each judged reasonable with zero blunders by a Sonnet reviewer with the fixed rubric) only on seeds 1-5, the seeds its rules were tuned on. On unseen seeds it fails: 1/5 clean at 45d4878 (seeds 11-55), 1/5 at d44c086 (seeds 101-505). The Warden fixed a first batch (4bbce43, f621913); its comment lists the blunders still open as leads.

Continue the review loop, but against seeds no earlier round has reviewed: each round, pick 5 new seeds (never 1-5, 11/22/33/44/55, 101/202/303/404/505 or any earlier round's), generate beryl_cli play expert expert --seed N, review each with a Sonnet subagent using by-b0q.3.5's rubric verbatim (plus format notes and the 29 cards' Oracle texts from data/cards.json), confirm each blunder in the transcript, and fix it as a general rule, not a seed-specific one. Record each round's seeds and verdicts as bead comments; commit no transcripts. Prefer rules that delete special cases; pin the riskiest new rules with tests in beryl_search/tests/expert.rs.

Acceptance criteria

  • Rubric revised to plausible play per epic design decision 6 (2026-11-05): a game is plausible if a competent human could plausibly have made each decision; a blunder is only an unambiguous, transcript-confirmed losing mistake; defensible judgment calls are not blunders. Record the revised rubric in a bead comment.
  • A final round of 10 seeds never reviewed before, chosen after the last code change, with at least 7 of 10 judged plausible (seeds and verdicts in a bead comment). If 3 rounds under the revised rubric all miss 7/10, the best round passes provided at least 5 of 10 are plausible; file each open blunder pattern as a non-blocking follow-up bead.
  • expert wins at least 90% of 200 seeded games against random and at least 70% against greedy.
  • New rules pinned by tests on hand-built positions where they guard a confirmed blunder.

Revised rubric (epic decision 6 as revised 2026-11-05), used verbatim by each Sonnet reviewer from round 8 on, with the same notes (transcript format, the 29 cards' Oracle texts):

You are reviewing one game of Magic: The Gathering between two AI players of the same WU Control deck. The transcript shows both hands for context, but judge each play only by what the deciding player could know. The question is whether a competent human cube player could plausibly have made each decision. List every blunder: only an unambiguous losing mistake that you can confirm in the transcript, one no competent player would make and that clearly costs the game or a card for nothing, such as missing an available lethal attack, not blocking or not answering a lethal attack while holding the answer and the mana for it, drawing or wheeling into an empty library, attacking into a block that loses a creature for nothing, or casting a spell into a counterspell the player has seen in the opponent's hand. Defensible judgment calls are never blunders: sequencing, choosing between reasonable targets or planeswalker abilities, holding or firing a wheel when behind, holding mana up or developing, playing around or into unseen cards. List questionable plays separately. End with a verdict: plausible (no blunders) or not plausible (one or more blunders).

Reviewers end with 'Verdict: plausible' or 'Verdict: not plausible'. Each blunder is confirmed in the transcript before it counts. Rounds: 10 never-reviewed seeds each, from 701 up, chosen after the last code change.
