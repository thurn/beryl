Close reason

Low memory frees full-size faces and pins Baseline; a lost surface keeps the device (doc corrected). staging a90e20ba0

Description

Read first: docs/beryl-3d.md 'Hosts' (the three behaviors: 'Losing the surface' and 'Low memory'), 'Card faces' ('Sizes and memory', 'Preparation') and 'Rendering' ('Choosing a tier'). Code: beryl_scene/src/faces.rs (Faces, FaceList, Faces::arrived, how a face with no layer draws as its placeholder), beryl_scene/src/client.rs (Client::handle: Input::Lifecycle falls to self.inputs.push and nothing reads it), beryl_render/src/faces.rs (FaceArray, FULL_LAYERS, the queue of full-size uploads), beryl_render/src/renderer.rs (upload_layer, upload_waiting, formats), beryl_render/src/surface.rs (attach, Surface), beryl_host/src/phone.rs (Phone::lifecycle, Phone::surface_lost, Phone::surface's Running arm) and beryl_host/src/desktop.rs (where it sends Lifecycle::LowMemory).

Why. The milestone's exit checks need the low-memory path and a surface lost and regained to work. Today a Lifecycle input changes nothing in the client or the renderer, and nothing checks what a regained surface keeps.

Decided:

  • A lost surface keeps the GPU device. wgpu's Device and Queue outlive the Surface on Metal, Vulkan and GL, so every uploaded face, glyph page and pipeline survives a suspend. On return the renderer makes a new Surface on the same device (Renderer::attach, which exists) and uploads nothing. Correct the doc's 'Losing the surface' paragraph, which says workers read the textures back from the disk cache: that is what a lost device would need, and a lost device is out of this milestone's scope (say so in the doc).
  • Low memory (Lifecycle::LowMemory): the client forgets every full-size face's layer (Faces drops each FaceSize::Full entry, so those faces draw at board size until asked for again, as the doc's 'Sizes and memory' says a face not yet uploaded does), and sets its tier to Baseline for the rest of the session (Tier::Baseline, so a later Input::Tier(Enhanced) is ignored after low memory). The renderer frees the full-size FaceArray's texture and makes it again, empty, at its next full-size upload; it frees the Enhanced tier's buffers when it has any (none exist before milestone 6: leave a method Renderer::low_memory(&mut self) that does the full-size array now, with a doc comment saying milestone 6's buffers are freed here too). Board-size faces and glyph pages are kept.
  • The renderer learns of low memory from its host, not from the frame: Phone::lifecycle and the desktop host's low-memory branch call renderer.low_memory() beside handing the input to the frame loop.

Tests:

  • beryl_scene, tests in src/faces.rs or tests/faces.rs: after two full-size faces and three board-size faces have arrived, LowMemory leaves the three board-size layers and no full-size layer, and the client's frame draws the two cards with their board-size layers; a later Input::Tier(Tier::Enhanced) leaves the tier Baseline.
  • beryl_render, tests/render.rs (it draws offscreen with Renderer::headless): after uploading two full-size layers, low_memory() makes renderer.uploaded(FaceSize::Full) (add this count if no equivalent exists) zero and a following full-size upload succeeds; the board-size count is unchanged. The surface-loss path cannot be drawn offscreen (a Surface needs a native window), so it is checked by the host beads' smoke tests, which suspend and resume the app and require frames after the return.
  • beryl_host: a test that Phone::lifecycle(LowMemory) before any surface is given is ignored without panicking.

Not in this bead: the Swift or Kotlin side of suspend or memory warnings (the host beads), the Enhanced tier.

Run: cd rust/beryl3d && cargo test -p beryl_scene -p beryl_render -p beryl_host; just b3d check verbs-zones.

Acceptance criteria

cargo test -p beryl_scene -p beryl_render -p beryl_host passes with tests showing: after LowMemory the client keeps board-size layers and no full-size layer and stays Baseline; Renderer::low_memory empties the full-size array and a later full-size upload succeeds; Phone::lifecycle before a surface is ignored. The doc's 'Losing the surface' and 'Low memory' say what was built.
