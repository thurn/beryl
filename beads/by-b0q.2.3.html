verdict ledger in regions.json (seeded from M1a, 93 pass/23 differs), ui-compare --review packets, changed-since-pass report, ui-verdict; M1b beads told to use them

Description

Part of Milestone 1 (epic by-b0q). Filed by the M1a Sage audit (by-b0q.2.2).

Measured problem

Pixel fidelity was the slowest part of M1a by far. by-b0q.1.26, .27, .28 and .31 and the pixel Warden by-b0q.1.29 took 95, 66, 78, 37 and 22 minutes of session time (about 300 minutes). That work ran from 08:44 to 10:59 UTC: 2.25 hours of a roughly 5-hour milestone. Every other executor bead took 5 to 56 minutes, and every other Warden took 5 to 17. Checks and tools were not the bottleneck: just ui-compare 'battlefield/*' takes about 5 s. The time went into reviewer rounds that did not converge:

  • Each pixel bead ran 5 or 6 rounds of fresh reviewer subagents. Verdicts flipped between rounds. On by-b0q.1.27, glow strength failed on one mockup after the other mockup's fix. On by-b0q.1.28, reviewers contradicted each other across rounds on the chevron and crescent curvature.
  • Each round's reviewer starts blind. Residuals and mockup conflicts that earlier rounds had already accepted got argued again. The by-b0q.1.29 Warden's three fresh reviewers were "far stricter than the beads' reviewers", flagging 2-10 px offsets on almost every part. The Warden then checked each claim against the side-by-sides by hand.
  • Parallel beads moved each other's passed parts (hand cards, land stacks, tray cards, graveyards) without a re-review. The Warden found this only by rendering every part at each bead's verdict commit and diffing. A measurement one bead left as a comment on another (1.31 to 1.28) was never acted on.
  • The verdicts exist only as long prose comments on closed beads.

M1b repeats this pattern with by-b0q.3.17, then .18 and .19 in parallel, then Warden .20, all on shared components.

Proposed change

Keep design decision 16: a fresh reviewer subagent's verdict, with no numeric thresholds. Make that verdict cheap to repeat and consistent:

  1. A committed verdict ledger in docs/ui_mockups/regions.json. Each part may carry verdict (pass or differs), the bead and commit, and accepted (residuals and mockup conflicts, one line each, with the bead that accepted them). Also store a fingerprint of the replica crop that passed, such as a hash of the downsampled crop. Seed the ledger from the per-part verdicts and accepted residuals and conflicts posted on by-b0q.1.26, .27, .28, .31 and .29.
  2. just ui-compare --review <glob> writes a reviewer packet per part: the existing side-by-side, onion and diff images plus a prompt.md. The prompt holds a fixed rubric (what "indistinguishable at a glance" means, what to ignore, such as masks, art windows and garbled mockup text, and how to answer) and the part's accepted lines, so a new reviewer does not raise them again. Every pixel bead and Warden then hands reviewers the same packet instead of writing its own prompt.
  3. A changed-since-verdict report. just ui-compare lists the parts marked pass whose replica crop no longer matches its stored fingerprint, so a bead sees at once when it moved a part another bead had passed.
  4. Comment on by-b0q.3.17, .18, .19 and .20 to use the packet, record each verdict and accepted residual in the ledger along with the bead comment, and record a contradiction between two reviewer rounds as an accepted conflict instead of running a third round on it.

Expected gain

Fewer reviewer rounds per part, with no re-arguing of accepted residuals, and Wardens that check the regression list instead of re-rendering old commits. Target: M1b's pixel beads average at or below M1a's non-pixel executor beads, about 30-40 minutes, instead of 66-95.

Acceptance criteria

  • regions.json parts can record a verdict (bead, commit), accepted residuals/conflicts and a fingerprint of the passing replica crop; the ledger is seeded from the verdicts on by-b0q.1.26/.27/.28/.31/.29.
  • just ui-compare --review <glob> writes per part the side-by-side, onion and diff images plus a prompt.md with a fixed rubric and that part's accepted lines.
  • just ui-compare reports parts marked pass whose replica crop no longer matches the stored fingerprint; a pytest covers the fingerprint match/mismatch and the packet contents.
  • by-b0q.3.17, .18, .19 and .20 each carry a comment telling them to review through the packet and record verdicts in the ledger.
  • just check passes; no screenshots or comparison outputs are committed.

Inline review (cold subagent): one confirmed problem: a ui-compare run with a page error left node's bare fingerprints.json in the cache, so the next ui-verdict crashed (KeyError) instead of asking for a re-render. Fixed: ui_compare deletes it on failure. It also confirmed determinism (fingerprint drift <=1 between identical runs, DRIFT=2) and sensitivity (a 2 px shift of a whole large part is reported). Deviation from the plan: the fingerprint is the 8x8 grid of mean replica colors (masks blacked out), compared with a 2-level tolerance, not an exact hash: WebKit's text antialiasing moves name bars by +-1 level between some runs, which flipped exact hashes on ~15 battlefield parts. Seed: verdicts from the comments on 1.26/.27/.28/.31/.29, each pass fingerprinted by rendering that bead's verdict commit (2f7cf8a, 44861e7, e915538, 636900b) with the new tooling, so today's ui-compare lists 23 passed parts moved since (hand cards, scry tray parts, sheet 05 stack parts, trigger you-angel/stack-trigger/cyan-arrow, opponent hand, and scry/view-battlefield-button, moved by 42920ed, which the 1.29 Warden's diff missed). 1.28's NOT YET parts are recorded differs; 1.29's flagged-but-unlisted parts keep their earlier verdict, since its comment does not name them.
