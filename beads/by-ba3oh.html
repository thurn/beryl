Beryl 3D fast-check steps start work the 180 s limit will kill

  • Description
  • Comments3
  • Changelog

Description

Symptom

Almost every fast check of a Beryl 3D change runs the full 180 s and is then cut off. In the 8 Beryl 3D fast checks between 06:25 and 06:55 UTC on 2026-11-16 (all on commits after 13ac73cc5), the record step (the shared test-profile build that every Beryl 3D lane starts with) took 57-158 s, or was itself cut off at 180 s in 2 runs. In all 5 runs where it finished, cargo-test and test were then cut off as well, and timeline-parity in 3. Between 04:30 and 06:10 UTC the pattern was the same: cargo-test was cut off in 12 of the 20 runs whose build finished. Each killed push waits the full 180 s, the CPU the killed steps spent is thrown away, and the Beryl 3D sweep runs the same work again later.

Why

  1. The steps after record do not fit their work to the time left. Rust tests (rust_tests.py, rust_fit.py), the oracles (oracle_selection.py), motion and the browser lanes read deadline.left() and plan less work, or skip it, when the run's target is near. The Beryl 3D steps (tools/src/beryl_tools/beryl3d/lane.py, cli.py run_cargo_test, run_shared_b3d, timeline.run_parity) start everything they were given, even 150 s into the run, when a step that needs 45-150 s cannot finish.
  2. The program steps run on starved CPU leases. A lease's size is fixed when it starts (tokens.run_program). After 04:30 UTC, b3d program leases got a median of 1 of the 12 cores they asked for, and held it for a median of 64 s. b3d test (every interface test, about 150 CPU-seconds) takes 11-13 s on all cores and 153 s in a 1-core lease. by-jyqd covers lease sizing; this bead is about the Beryl 3D steps choosing their work.

Work

  • Before starting, each of cargo-test, test and timeline-parity reads deadline.left(). It runs only the part whose predicted time fits, or defers the whole step to the Beryl 3D sweep at once (exit as deferred, as a step cut off at the limit is), instead of starting work the hard limit will kill. Predict from the step records the run log already holds (step_seconds on each step's first record; deadline.Records).
  • Where a step can be cut down rather than skipped, cut it down. b3d test takes a name prefix, so it could run the tests for the recordings the change touches first. nextest can run only the changed crates' tests (cargo-test -p). timeline-parity --fixtures could take a subset.
  • Consider whether the fast check should run Beryl 3D's post-build steps at all when the shared build takes over about 120 s. The sweep already runs every step on staging.

Done

Over a day of Beryl 3D fast checks, the share of runs that reach the 180 s limit falls from nearly all to under a quarter, and the median Beryl 3D fast check ends within 120 s, with deferred steps named in the output as going to the Beryl 3D sweep.

Files: tools/src/beryl_tools/beryl3d/lane.py, beryl3d/cli.py, beryl3d/timeline.py, deadline.py, rust_fit.py (the pattern to follow), tools/tests/test_beryl3d_cli.py. Measure with runs.jsonl (script check, lanes beryl3d-*, records with timeout).
