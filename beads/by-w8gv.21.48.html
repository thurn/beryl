Decisions (executor). Both fixes are one change of what a vanish's step is.

Before: the step held the card's going in its first 0.7 (tracks.ts GONE) and the closing-up of what it left room for in the rest, so with nothing to close up the last 0.3 was empty table, and the card was at its new place only after that. Now the step is the card's going alone: the dissolve and its effect last the whole step, the card is at its new place as the step ends (as it is gone), and what it left room for closes up after the step, for CLOSES (0.4) of the step's length in whole ms, on tracks of their own (Track.closes, tracks.ts roomIn). A beat is not over while such a track runs (closedBy, asked as the beat would end), so the next beat waits for a row to close and for nothing where none does. Decided when the beat would end and not as the step begins, because of a batch: of two cards that leave together the second first gets a closing-up track from the first's step and then leaves where it lay, and nothing closes.

Why not end the step early or stretch the crumble inside the old step: by motion holds every step to its verb's duration (motion.py timing_verdicts), so a step's length must be the data's alone, never the layout's. So the data now says what is played: vanish's durations in data/ui-conventions.json are the time the card takes to go, 0.7 of what they were (default 565 to 395, destroyed 965 to 675, countered 910 to 635, resolved 500 to 350, exiled 600 to 420, sacrificed 530 to 370), which is the going the review's rounds 1 to 3 saw and passed line by line; bounced is 300, not 260, the glow's own time, so nothing of the halo is left on the card in its hand (the verb suite holds that). The values had been Arena's medians; they are Beryl's own now. A countered spell's counter waits its whole step (timing.ts stagger), as it waited GONE of it.

Pile: the stage tells a layout what is still leaving (StageLayout.rest's second argument, from Entry.leaving, not a Table field: it is the stage's knowledge, not the script's replay). The phone's top of a pile is the last card that has arrived; the desktop passes them as game/layout.ts's borrowed cards, which that layout already lays out so (the pile lies as it did, the card keeps its place on top for when it lands). The desktop had the same fault when two cards left for one graveyard together (the old top was two down, drawn as a paper edge), and no longer.

Walkers: stage.step is null while a row closes up after a step, with the stage still busy. verbs.mjs waits through it and takes the next step's before-sample with everything closed up; film-walk.mjs and stepped.mjs no longer count that time as idle.

stage.ts was at 800 lines: poseAt moved to tracks.ts as poseOn, with closedBy.
