Sweeps at utility priority run 20-60x slower than their CPU time

Close reason

Sweeps take sweep-class CPU tokens (a quarter of the pool) at normal priority instead of utility QoS; d89dca478

Description

Symptom: the browser sweep's wall time grew from 313-881 s (2026-11-14) to 6,317 s and 12,364 s on 2026-11-15, longer than its 3-hour interval, so firings skip and failures are found later. The Beryl 3D sweep shows the same: on 5790e744, b3d-fmt took 1,768 s wall for 28 CPU-seconds and b3d-clippy 5,553 s wall for 125 CPU-seconds. A ui-test bisection on 602294c2 took 13,056 s, past bisect's 1,800 s search budget.

Cause: sweep.py, beryl3d/sweep.py and nightly.py run everything through system.utility (taskpolicy -c utility). On this Mac at load 40-70 the utility class gets almost no CPU while agents' fast checks run, so work that needs minutes of CPU takes hours of wall time. Measured from $BERYL_CACHE/ci/runs.jsonl, scripts sweep, b3d-sweep and bisect, wall seconds against cpu_seconds per step.

1a224723 made browser waits at low priority tolerate this (3-minute Playwright waits), which stopped the false flaky-check beads, but not the lateness.

Done means: a sweep finishes within its 3-hour interval at the machine's usual load. Options to measure: run sweeps at default QoS but through the CPU token pool (tokens.py) with a small lease, so they get a bounded, guaranteed share instead of leftovers; or give the bisection its own deadline in CPU-seconds rather than wall seconds. Compare sweep wall time and fast-check span before and after.

Derek Thurn2026-11-16

Fixed in d89dca478. Sweeps (browser and Beryl 3D) and their bisections now run at normal priority and take CPU tokens of a new lowest class, sweep, which holds at most a quarter of the pool (4 of 18 cores here) and yields to any waiting higher class. Slow CI starts them outside its step's lease (tokens.unheld, coverage.detached_sweep).

Choices made: a new class below ci, not ci itself, so a sweep never slows the slow CI that gates master; ci leases waited about 13 s on average over 2026-11-14/15, so starvation behind ci waiters is short. The bisection keeps its wall-second budget (1,800 s), because at normal priority wall and CPU time stay close. The nightly, tournaments and coverage refresh stay at utility QoS: they have no interval to meet.

Baseline to compare (runs.jsonl, wall/CPU per step summed per day): sweep 7.0 (11-14), 22.4 (11-15), 14.0 (11-16); b3d-sweep 6.9 and 6.1; slow CI 0.6-1.0. Check the sweep's log.jsonl seconds and push.wall-seconds in just efficiency over the next days.
