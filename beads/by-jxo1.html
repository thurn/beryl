Reproduced: the hand-hover raise is a copy (ui/src/stage/raised.ts) that appeared and vanished with the hover, so both rise and release were one-frame snaps (stage.css said 'it is there at once').

Arena, filmed whole at 30 fps in three recordings: jD7VqaT8VC8 rise 45:26.77 (~100-130 ms, ~60% in frame 1), release 45:27.00 (~130-160 ms, ~50% in frame 1); SswBO8aOOgc rise 21:01.95 (~70-100 ms), release 21:02.75 (~200 ms); IEXcjBn3BXk rise 18:06.62 (~67 ms). Every one eases out. Moving to a neighbor: the old card drops while the new one rises (SswBO8aOOgc 21:02.55).

Fix: the copy eases on browser frames (requestAnimationFrame, like the heft in dom.ts), through --raise-k (0 = in the fan, 1 = raised) in stage.css. Rise takes 100 ms and drop 160 ms, both cubic ease-out (raised.ts RISE_MS/DROP_MS, raisedAt). A dropping copy sits under the newly hovered card's copy. If the pointer comes back it rises again from where it is. It is removed at once if the card is held (dom.ts raise passes atOnce), or if its node moves or is removed (this covers a cast or a drag). Reduced motion keeps the snap. The node keeps its hit box, so the hover never drops.

Side-by-side (whole table, Arena over Beryl, rise row then drop row, scratch pair.png): Beryl's card is about 60% of the way up at 40 ms and fully up by about 104 ms. On the drop it is partway down at 40 and 72 ms and back in the fan by about 168 ms. Both match Arena within a frame. Differences left: (1) size. Arena's hand card grows to about 2x while Beryl's lifts and scales 1.06, with an inspector panel at the side. That is lane bead by-q2hn's question (previews on hand hover), so I left it alone here. (2) Depth while moving to a neighbor: Beryl keeps the newly hovered card in front. The recording is ambiguous about which card Arena draws on top.

Tests: scripts/stage-raised.test.mjs pins the timing curve against Arena's frames. The kept check 'a hovered hand card eases up out of the fan and back down as the pointer leaves' samples the copy every 8 ms and requires positions between rest and raised on the way up and on the way down. It FAILED on the old code and passes now. The other hover checks pass, as do 'visible strip' (moving across neighbors), 'pass-on button' and the crowded raised-card checks. data/ui-conventions.json 'Hover inspection' now has the measured timing and evidence.
