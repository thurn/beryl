Description

Long-think MCTS: a known-strong reference agent

Can mcts-hidden become an obviously strong player if we let it think arbitrarily long per decision? Shipping a multi-minute-per-move agent is not the point: having an agent we know is very strong is useful on its own, as a teacher and a yardstick for tuning and training other models (the evaluator and value model of docs/ai-strategy.md §5). The run is open-ended: it keeps climbing the budget and adding games, and stays readable whenever we stop it.

Decisions (design interview, 2026-11-05)

  • D1 Mirror: cb8d5d5f only (decks::WU_CONTROL). Start only once the baselines are in shape: greedy retuned (by-0w2.17) and the expert retuned for the new cards and frozen. Greedy is the rollout policy, so its flaws are bias that more rollouts cannot average away: before the retune greedy decks itself (greedy v random 75.5%, expert v greedy 60%, expert v random 86% over 400 games; the Vintage mirror gives 96.5%, 83%, 98%).
  • D2 Budget ladder, open-ended: rungs of 3k, 10k, 30k, 100k, 300k, 1M rollouts per decision, adding x3 rungs as long as strength keeps climbing. 60 games (30 seat-alternated seed pairs) per rung against an opponent before the next rung leads; then keep adding pairs. Same seeds on every rung, so rungs compare pair by pair.
  • D3 Sight: mcts-hidden on every rung; mcts-omniscient at the top rung reached, as a diagnostic of whether hidden information has become the bottleneck.
  • D4 Searched decisions: a priority decision with the deciding player's own spell or ability on top of the stack passes without search (27 of 159 decisions per seat per game; the expert never acts there). Everything else is searched, including empty-stack end and combat steps: that is where the deck flashes Wandering Emperor, Subtlety and Snapcaster (0.56 a game for the expert).
  • D5 Speed-ups before the run: an exact stop in rounds (moves identical to the full budget) and subtree reuse within one action. Deterministic: a seed still reproduces a move. 18 trees, as in Milestone 1.
  • D6 No end state and no pre-registered bar. We watch the streamed summary and stop when we've learned enough. Once a rung beats the frozen expert 85%+ over 60 games the expert no longer resolves strength, so that rung also plays the rung below it (an Elo chain).
  • D7 Every searched decision's root statistics are kept, so the games double as training data for other models.

Cost (measured 2026-11-05, busy machine)

  • Rollout benchmark: ~0.575 ms a rollout (~1,740 per core-second), 135 decisions a rollout, next + apply 4.0 µs a decision. With early-game rollouts being longer, assume ~0.65 ms.
  • 400 expert mirror games: 24 turns, 159 decisions per seat a game (p10 81, p90 233); 132 searched under D4.
  • 18 cores ~ 12 fast-core equivalents while a decision waits on its slowest tree. 100k rollouts ~ 65 core-s ~ 5.4 s a decision today.
  • Per game, today -> with D5 (est. 40% fewer rollouts): 100k ~12 -> ~7 min; 300k ~36 -> ~22 min; 1M ~2 h -> ~72 min. The 3k-30k rungs take ~3 h for 60 games each; then 100k and 300k reach 60 games each in ~1.5 days.
