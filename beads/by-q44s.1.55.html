Close reason

obsolete: the 344 MB is Metal's per-encoder driver pools, held while frames draw, not a Beryl transient; per-frame blit pool filed as by-q44s.1.58

Description

Symptom: beryl3d --window none --for 8s (release build with the tools feature, 2026-11-15, Apple M5 Max) reports a physical footprint of 360 MB from its first sample at 80 ms until about 1.3 s, of which 344 MB is 'Owned physical footprint (unmapped) (graphics)' in footprint <pid>. After that the footprint is 37 MB, 24 MB of it the GPU's. /usr/bin/time -l gives a peak memory footprint of 377 to 379 MB on five runs. docs/beryl-3d.md 'Card faces' allows about 32 MB of face textures for a match, and the floor (decision C2) includes the iPhone 6s with 2 GB of memory, where a 344 MB transient could get the app killed.

Where to look: Renderer::new and set_faces in rust/beryl3d/crates/beryl_render/src/renderer.rs and face_arrays / FaceArray::new in src/faces.rs (the placeholder writes for every layer, the full-size array of FULL_LAYERS layers at 672 by 936), the glyph atlas (src/atlas.rs), and the app's startup in rust/beryl3d/crates/beryl_3d/src/app.rs. Sample footprint <pid> every few milliseconds over the first 1.5 s to find which allocation it is.

Measured in by-q44s.1.16; the doc's 'Measurements' section names this bead beside the number.

Acceptance criteria

The app's peak physical footprint at startup, with no window, is within 32 MB of its settled footprint, measured with footprint or /usr/bin/time -l on five runs; the doc's 'Measurements' section gives the new peak and drops this bead's name.

Derek Thurn2026-11-15

Finding: the 344 MB is not a Beryl allocation and not a startup transient. Probes (footprint before and after each step of beryl3d's startup and Renderer::render_view) show Beryl's own state at about 9 MB until the first queue.submit, which jumps to 360 MB. A bare Swift Metal program reproduces it: the first render pass in a process costs about 185 MB whatever the target size (16x16: 180 MB, 1024: 185 MB, 4096: 265 MB), the first blit encoder about 112 MB more, an empty command buffer nothing. The driver frees each pool about 1 s after the last encoder of its kind. The default '--window none' run draws one frame and then idles, which is why its footprint fell to 37 MB after 1.3 s. A run that keeps drawing (--fixture verbs-zones) sits at 400 MB for its whole length, so peak and settled footprint are the same once 'settled' means while drawing. The acceptance as written (peak within 32 MB of the idle footprint) cannot be met by any client that draws a frame, so I am closing this as obsolete. What Beryl can cut: wgpu encodes every queue.write_buffer as a blit, and render_view writes uniforms and records each frame, so the 112 MB blit pool never frees; filed by-q44s.1.58 to draw frames without blits and to correct the doc's Measurements (blocked on by-q44s.1.16, which is still writing that section). Not known: the pools' size on the floor's phones; they likely scale with the GPU, which I could not test (agents install on the iPhone only when the user asks).
