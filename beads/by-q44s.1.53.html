Close reason

stress-1024 3.2 s (was 1,740 s), all tests 75 CPU-s/16 s (was 5,200/29 min); 10 ms a test, not 2

Description

just b3d test (beryl_inspect's runner module and cli/test.rs) runs every interface test: 7,336 of them (1,834 recordings, each from the seats it is recorded for, in the four desktop windows of pages::DESKTOP_WINDOWS). docs/beryl-3d.md 'Test speed' targets 2 ms of CPU a test and under 10 s for all of them before a push. Measured on 2026-11-15 in the debug build just b3d uses (load 30 to 60, five runs; the medians are in the doc's 'Test speed' table): about 5,200 CPU-seconds and 29 minutes on the clock for the whole run. That is far over the fast check's 180 s step limit, so test is not a step of the beryl3d lane yet.

Where the time goes:

  • b3d:stress-1024/* (4 tests) take about 1,740 s each on the clock, roughly 60% of all test time, and set the wall time of the whole run. A sample of the process shows the reachability check (agent/hit.rs: in_quad, covers, Grid::reaches, catches, free_cells) and driver/check.rs::unique (a Vec::contains scan over every fault) on top: both grow with the square of the number of cards. stress-255 takes about 24 s a test.
  • Every other test averages about 0.28 CPU-seconds, about 140 times the 2 ms target. A test plays its recording twice (Page::check_all: once to record the scripts, once to evaluate frames), draws a frame at each check time and at every tick while a judged step plays ("every step shown" samples its subject every tick in both modes, which doubled the run's CPU time but is needed for the check times to find what every tick finds: see beryl_inspect/tests/differential.rs).
  • The build is unoptimized except for the packages [profile.dev.package.*] in rust/beryl3d/Cargo.toml lists. cargo test uses [profile.test] opt-level = 1, and there the 1,000 differential plantings (each checked at the check times and on every tick) take 13 s, which suggests an optimized build is much faster.

Bring the cost down without cutting tests or raising a budget (docs/checks.md, AGENTS.md "Checks"): make the reachability check and the fault dedupe sub-quadratic, measure what an optimization level for beryl_scene and beryl_inspect costs in edit-to-output time (doc 'Edit-to-output time': 3 s for just b3d tree) against what it saves here, and remove whatever else a profile of one ordinary test shows. Keep the differential test passing: the check times must find every fault every tick finds. Measure as the doc's 'Test speed' asks (median of five runs with the load) and update its table. When the run fits the fast check, say so in a comment on by-q44s.1.54, the bead that adds the step to the lane.

Acceptance criteria

The four stress-1024 tests each take under a tenth of their 2026-11-15 time and no interface check is quadratic in the number of cards; every test at its check times takes a median of at most 2 ms of CPU, or the bead says what was tried and what each step saved; the differential test still passes; the doc's 'Test speed' table has the new medians with the load.

What each step saved, measured with the runner commit 7606a63f8 (bead by-q44s.1.40, not yet on staging) applied on top of this branch in a scratch tree, full b3d test of 7,552 tests:

  • Before (bead's figure): 5,200 CPU-s, 29 min on the clock. stress-1024 about 1,740 s a test.
  • Reachable check sub-quadratic (spatial index Overlaps built once per frame, candidates limited to the card's subtree and what is drawn above it, first-fit search) plus a set for the fault dedupe: stress-255 went from 14 CPU-s to 1.4, and stress-1024 from 1,740 s to 10 s on the clock, unoptimized.
  • Plus beryl_scene and beryl_inspect at opt-level 1: 270 CPU-s, 44 s. At opt-level 2: 114 CPU-s, 17 s. The rebuild after a one-line beryl_scene edit stayed about 2 s (3 runs each, load 50 to 100). Optimizing beryl_session, beryl_table and beryl_protocol too saved nothing more, so it is not kept.
  • Recordings, views and card records shared behind Arc between plays, Responses queuing indices, exact fast paths in covered and has_free_rect: 91 CPU-s.
  • No copy of the recording in pages::page / Page::new, views deserialized from &Value: 75 CPU-s, 16 s on the clock (median of five, load 300 to 330). stress-1024 is about 3.2 s a test.
  • Tried and dropped: skipping the frames between check times while something animates (Wake::Now jumps straight to the target). It found identical faults on every test and saved nothing, because the frames at check times are the cost. The 2 ms a test target is not met: about 10 CPU-ms a test. The profile puts about 40% in opening the page (parsing the recording once and its views once per window), about 25% in the client handling views (script compile, flat-layer layout, text shaping about 13%), and about 10% building draw lists the checks never read. Faults were identical to the base on every test at every step; the differential test passes (5.7 s, down from 13 s). The runner commit already parses recordings outside the cache lock in recordings::find, so this branch leaves that alone.

Derek Thurn2026-11-15

A finding for this bead from by-q44s.1.40: the check times are no cheaper than every tick for the whole run. Five runs at the check times (load 25 to 55): median 4,955 CPU-s, 1,381 s on the clock, 715,100 frames. One run on every tick (load 70 to 300): 4,927 CPU-s, 1,206 s, 1,144,020 frames. The stress-1024 tests draw 152 frames each at their check times and 121 on every tick, because times() in beryl_scene/src/driver/check.rs adds the start, middle and end of every segment and every step, and 1,024 cards' segments start at slightly different times. Rounding the check times to the tick grid (or capping them at one per tick) would make a test never draw more frames than every tick does; check with the differential test that nothing is lost. The a sample (360 tests, 5 runs each way): 91 CPU-ms a test at the check times, 110 on every tick.
