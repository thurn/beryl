Derek Thurn2026-11-11

Deviations from the plan's K6 and K7 as milestone 3 built them, recorded by the M3 Mason (by-w8gv.6.1) so they are in one place. Each was decided on a child bead; none needs undoing.

  1. The kernel holds more than the plan's Crates row lists: the decision types, Profile, Event and Sink, the chance chokepoints, GameTable, Observation and filter evaluation, because State embeds them or a chokepoint takes them (by-w8gv.5.1, decision 2). The contract in beryl_kernel/src/lib.rs is the accurate list; the Archivist should take the Crates row from it.
  2. "Only the kernel writes a field" has two sanctioned handles that write raw: the board door (State::board, for scenarios, checkpoints, tools and tests) and the legacy accessor (State::legacy_mut, deleted by milestone 4). The door is held out of rules code by convention today and by a check once by-w8gv.6.2 lands. One &mut leaves the crate: Rederive::results, the derived table lent to the layers while stale.
  3. "A bitboard outside the kernel offers only order-free operations" holds, with one unnamed-by-the-rules order left: candidate order (ascending slot), which Choice indices in saves and rollouts depend on. Its 18 walks in beryl_engine are held by a comment each, read by the Warden; milestone 5 rewrites most of those sites.
  4. "The two-player shortcut is not exposed" is true of rules code and card bodies only: beryl_kernel::two_player::TwoPlayerSeat is public for search, session and tools, and the two-player check keeps it out of the rules (by-w8gv.5.13).
  5. Capacities are layout parameters, but Small was not widened: 80-card decks play only in Large (by-w8gv.5.17, revisit in by-4q5y). Seat stays a two-variant enum in beryl_card, so a third player needs a third seat first.
