Rules for every shape

  • The session holds the prompt's state (K26). The UI draws the marks and button states it is sent and sends clicks as commands through ui/src/ipc.ts. No prompt logic in the UI: nothing there decides what is eligible, selected or allowed.
  • Wording is Arena's exactly and comes from the session (K22; AGENTS.md, UI and Workflow). Exception 3 stands: never ask a question before a choice the next screen can decline.
  • Cards in a prompt are the stage's own nodes, moved by verbs: one visual per object, no borrowed copies.
  • Checks: shape:<name>/<seat>/<layout> in ui/scripts/shapes.mjs (made by by-w8gv.21.26, reporting and selected as ui/scripts/verbs.mjs is), from both seats and in both layouts: the chooser's seat answers the prompt by clicks, the other seat sees what an opponent may see. One check per shape with its purposes as cases, never one per card. A hand-written text snapshot per shape (K24) of the marks and buttons after each click, in beryl_session's tests if milestone 6 wrote none.
  • Today's files are the list of cases; the cut-over deletes them.

Decisions and deviations (by-w8gv.21.29, executor).

  1. Snapshots. Milestone 6 wrote the hand-written text of both shapes after each click (beryl_session tests/payments.rs: crew, waterbend, plans, mana by hand; tests/declarations.rs: attack, walker, block, twins, a token made attacking). None added, as by-w8gv.21.27 decided for its shapes.
  2. One outline at a time in a payment. The session marks what Auto Pay would tap suggested and what else can pay eligible; UI Conventions asks for no proposal shown until Auto Pay is hovered or held, and by-4hjm and by-z973 asked why there were two and three outline colours. So while a pay prompt is open (data-pay on the board's root, stage.css) a suggested mark is drawn as the eligible outline, and while the pointer is over Auto Pay, or a touch is held on it 450 ms, the suggested alone show, in their ember ring, and the eligible outline is put out. A touch held only shows; its release presses nothing (as the old PaymentButton). The marks in the stage's model and on the page are the session's throughout: only the look of two of them is the screen's. The convention's entry says so now and lists all nine reports.
  3. The amount left. state.left is written in parentheses after what the prompt asks, "Tap permanents to Waterbend ({3})", as by-4hjm asked ("the text should say Tap permanents to Waterbend (3)"), and counts down with the picks. The same for a crew cost and a payment of mana.
  4. The paying mark lasts through the tap (by-w8gv.21.22's gotcha). A permanent picked to pay lies turned under its mark. prompt.ts no longer ends a paying mark before the script that answers the payment where that script taps the permanent: the end goes into the beat after the last that taps one (paid()). tracks.ts paying(): a permanent tapped under its mark lies as it rests from the tap's first tick, so it never stands up to turn again. No verb's values changed and no moment of the verb suite plays this (verbs.mjs shuts the prompts), so there is nothing for just motion --film to film and no motion review was run; the pay case holds it at every tick (tapThatPays), and I broke it on purpose to see the case fail.
  5. The line to the pointer. UI Conventions, "Attacking a planeswalker" (Arena: "an arrow follows the cursor to the planeswalker"). The session holds no pointer, so ui/src/stage/Aim.tsx draws a line in the combat line's look from each creature in state.aiming to the mouse, under the cards, on the desktop board only (a touch has no pointer to follow). It is not a span of the stage and no part of the prompt's state. The aim itself is the session's as by-w8gv.11.11 built it: a click on the creature, then a click on the player or the planeswalker (an attacker does not default to the player while a planeswalker can be attacked; by-w8gv.11.15 has that judgement).
  6. Long titles. The crew prompt asks in a sentence of Arena's rules text and has three buttons, which ran the desktop dock up under the stack and was cut off in the phone's one-line pill (the shots' audit caught the second). A title of more than 40 letters is set at 18 px and 110 px wider than the buttons on the desktop, and wraps in the phone's pill beside the prompt's other buttons.
  7. Fixtures. shape-pay ends at the scry prompt its waterbend leads to (the scry is the split shape's case). shape-declare blocks first and attacks on the turn after, because a click fixture's board must have played its scripts out by the stop: the opponent's attack is scripted, the human's block and attack are clicks. Its board has a card in hand because the phone's check reads one by a touch.
  8. Cases not in the fixtures, drawn by the same code: convoke (no card in the pool asks it), mana paid one source at a time (no game of the app asks it), the aim alone of a creature made attacking (attack-token's board; part aim, no buttons), All Attack with no planeswalker to aim at. A payment plan the session does not hold arrives with no state and is still answered over the table by the old screen, as is the division of damage (by-w8gv.21.28), for which PhoneStageBoard keeps the old PhoneDock.
  9. The suite's pointer is a mouse on both layouts: the phone's long press on Auto Pay is built and not checked.
  10. An older no-browser test used the pay shape as its example of a shape the stage does not hold; it uses number now.
