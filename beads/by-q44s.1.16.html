Description

Read first: docs/beryl-3d.md 'Measurements' and 'Threads', and docs/benchmarking.md ('Timing on the Mac', 'Old against new: the ABBA harness').

This is the last bead of milestone 1. The doc's 'Measurements' section holds numbers from the prototype. Replace them with numbers from Beryl 3D itself.

First, bring the recorded fixtures under the gates: add verbs-zones and pay-default to the gated boards in data/beryl3d/gates.json, for the native gate and the instruction-count gate, with limits set from measurement. pay-default opens prompts, so its worst frame is one in which handle lays out a prompt bar and its text with taffy and parley. Run the count gate twice and require identical counts.

Then measure, each with the load beside it and the median of at least five runs:

  • instructions per frame, worst and total, for verbs-zones, pay-default, stress-255 and stress-1024, and for pay-default how many of the worst frame's instructions are taffy's and how many parley's;
  • time per Client::frame natively on the same boards;
  • offscreen frame time through the renderer (CPU time to encode and submit, and the time to a finished frame) for stress-255 at 1920 by 1080;
  • draw calls, instances, upload bytes, blended area and fragments shaded on the same boards;
  • the app's stripped binary size, its peak memory in a game, and the time from launch to the first presented frame;
  • face preparation times and texture memory, from the card faces bead;
  • the frame delivery summaries, from the frame delivery bead;
  • the two edit-to-output times, from the tool's beads.

Add frame_abba to beryl_inspect/benches in the pattern of rust/crates/beryl_bench/benches/rollout_abba.rs, timing Client::frame old against new, for later optimization work.

Compare each number with the doc's budgets ('Performance gates', 'Rendering', 'Card faces', 'Threads'). Where a budget is not met, say so in the doc beside the number and file a bead under milestone 1.

Last, delete the prototype: rust/prototypes/beryl3d, the proto3d recipe in the justfile, and its code in tools/src/beryl_tools/app.py, in a commit of its own. Remove the epic's rule that names it as a fallback.

Run: just b3d gate; just b3d gate --counts; just b3d gate --gpu; just b3d cargo bench -p beryl_inspect --bench frame_abba -- --quick.

What the prototype found (rust/prototypes/beryl3d, 2026-11-14; read its code for a working example, then build it properly):

  • Time frames paced at 60 frames a second, as a window draws them. Back to back, stress-255's core time was 12 µs and its encode and submit 36 µs; paced, 42 µs and 354 µs, because the clocks drop between frames. Every frame timing here is taken paced (sleeping to the next tick, or on a display link), never back to back.
  • GPU timestamps do not work on this Mac; give the wall-clock time from submit to finish, labelled as such.
  • The prototype's --stats output is the format to beat: median and worst of five runs, with the load.

Acceptance criteria

The doc's Measurements section is Beryl 3D's own numbers with loads; every budget in the doc is marked met or has a bead; verbs-zones and pay-default are gated with identical counts on two runs; frame_abba runs; the prototype is deleted.

From by-q44s.1.55: the 344 MB startup figure is Metal driver memory (a ~185 MB pool for the first render pass and ~112 MB for the first blit encoder, each freed ~1 s after its last use), not a transient Beryl allocation. A run that keeps drawing (--fixture verbs-zones) holds ~400 MB throughout; the idle default run frees it after one frame. If the Measurements text is not pushed yet, please describe it that way; by-q44s.1.58 (blocked on this bead) corrects it otherwise.
