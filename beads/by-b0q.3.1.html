Description

Part of Milestone 1 (epic by-b0q): a rules-exact engine, game UI and AI opponents for a mirror match of the 40-card WU Control main deck in docs/sample-decks/2026-10-17-PrinceJools-wu-control.txt (29 distinct cards). Read the epic's description (plan, card table) and design field (decisions) first; docs/engine-architecture.md is the design. Rules code cites the Comprehensive Rules with yawgatog links and quotations (AGENTS.md).

Design decision 4 and architecture section 19.1 (D21).

  • beryl_search: the rollout policy. Per-game card-value scores indexed by game-table index (hand-tuned for the 29 cards and tokens, with a fallback from mana value and stats); cast or activate the highest-value affordable option; target the opponent's most valuable permanent; counter a spell worth more than a threshold; attack when no untapped potential blocker can kill the attacker; block to prevent lethal or to trade up; chump only to prevent lethal; answer mid-resolution choices inline. No copies, no lookahead, no allocation. Registered as greedy through an Agent wrapper.
  • beryl_cli: play (a seeded game between named agents, writing a transcript), tournament (round robin, N games per pairing, seats alternated, games in parallel across cores with derived seeds, results with Wilson 95% intervals as JSON and a table), and the transcript format: omniscient text, each turn both hands, both boards (P/T, counters, tapped), life and library sizes, and each non-trivial decision with the decider, its kind, the candidates in short labels and the choice.
  • just play and just tournament recipes.
  • Scale the fuzz run: 10,000 random-versus-random mirror games through the CLI.

Acceptance criteria

  • greedy wins at least 90% of 200 seeded games against random.
  • 10,000 random mirror games: no invariant failure, stall rate under 0.5%; numbers in a comment on the epic.
  • A test asserts zero allocations in the rollout policy over a game.
  • A sample transcript reads clearly (attach an excerpt as a bead comment, not a file).

What it does: beryl_search gets RolloutPolicy (values.rs: hand-tuned values for the 29 cards, an estimate from mana value/stats/loyalty for tokens and anything else, plus flags for wheels, helpful-target abilities (Emperor +1) and never-activate abilities (Iceberg waterbend); rollout.rs: the rules), an Inline answer source, rollout() (the inlined loop), and GreedyAgent registered as "greedy". beryl_cli gets play (transcript) and tournament (round robin, seats alternated, games across cores via scoped threads and an atomic job index, per-game seeds derived from the tournament seed, Wilson 95% table and --json, --check for invariants each decision). just play / just tournament run the release build from the caller's cwd.

Acceptance:

  • greedy v random 200 seeded games: 192/200 = 96.0% [92.3, 98.0] (seed 1); 189/200 = 94.5% (seed 2). tests/greedy.rs pins 40 seeded games at >= 90%.
  • 10,000 random mirror games: 0 invariant failures, 0 stalls; numbers on the epic.
  • tests/allocations.rs now also counts allocations over 8 full rollouts of the mirror under the policy with inline answers: 0.
  • Transcript excerpt below. Also added tests/greedy.rs::inline_answers_replay_to_the_same_game, the §17.4 differential: inline answers recorded, replayed through Replay pauses, same outcome and state hash.

Decisions and deviations:

  • AgentInfo::new now takes &GameTable (the policy's values are per game). GameTable derives Clone so GreedyAgent owns a copy (agents outlive the borrow). Observation::matches(t, filter, slot) added so the policy can test target filters on public objects.
  • The policy is stateless: BlockTarget re-derives the blocker being declared (ours, BLOCKING, combat None) and re-scores, so it works in replay/MCTS/determinizations.
  • Win rate counts wins only; draws and stalls are reported separately.
  • Harmful vs helpful targets: a spec whose filter says Whose(You), or the hand list, is helpful; everything else is aimed at the opponent. Removal waits for a target worth 25, counters for a spell worth 40, so Strip Mine (lands cap at 15 on the battlefield) is effectively never activated. Fine for D21; the expert system owns card-specific play.
  • Bug found and fixed while reading transcripts: the CLI and the search tests' play() showed agents the committed pre-resolution state at a paused body; they now observe through view() as sessions already did (Jace's loot had discarded the just-drawn card as unknown).
  • Not measured here: the 300 ns/decision budget; that is the rollout benchmark bead's.
