Description

Part of Milestone 1 (epic by-b0q): a rules-exact engine, game UI and AI opponents for a mirror match of the 40-card WU Control main deck in docs/sample-decks/2026-10-17-PrinceJools-wu-control.txt (29 distinct cards). Read the epic's description (plan, card table) and design field (decisions) first; docs/engine-architecture.md is the design. Rules code cites the Comprehensive Rules with yawgatog links and quotations (AGENTS.md).

Design decision 6. Register it as expert.

  • beryl_search: prioritized rules with card knowledge for the 29 cards (counterspell discipline for Mana Drain, No More Lies and Mana Tithe; when to cast Time Spiral and Timetwister; Strip Mine targets; Teferi, Jace and Emperor lines; impending versus full Overlord; Swift Reconfiguration as removal; holding flash threats), using engine copies only for combat math and lethal checks. It decides from its Observation only.
  • The review loop: generate 5 expert-versus-expert transcripts with beryl_cli play from fixed seeds (record the seeds in a bead comment). Give each to a subagent with model: sonnet and this rubric:

You are reviewing one game of Magic: The Gathering between two AI players of the same WU Control deck. The transcript shows both hands for context, but judge each play only by what the deciding player could know. List every blunder: an error a competent cube player would not make, such as missing lethal, letting a game-deciding threat resolve while holding an answer and the mana for it, spending the only counterspell on a minor spell before a major threat, attacking into a block that loses a creature for nothing, not blocking a lethal attack, choosing a clearly worse planeswalker ability, or refilling the opponent's hand with a wheel while far behind. List questionable plays separately. End with a verdict: reasonable or not reasonable.

  • Fix and repeat until the bar is met. Post the final verdicts as a bead comment; commit no transcripts.

Acceptance criteria

  • All 5 games judged reasonable, with zero blunders, in the final review.
  • expert wins at least 90% of 200 seeded games against random.
  • Tests pin a few card-knowledge rules on hand-built positions.
