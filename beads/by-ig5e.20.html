CPU sampler still credits minutes with more CPU than the machine has

Close reason

Fixed in 4d4ba08c9: children reaped mid-read no longer double-counted; over-core minutes 11/54 -> 1/54 (18.5 cores at load 90, timing jitter)

Description

After the zombie double-count fix (by-ig5e.18, 16c07b632; sampler restarted on jarvis 2026-11-15 20:02 UTC), the cpu_minutes rows in the efficiency store still show minutes over the 18 cores: 12 of 54 minutes between 21:50 and 22:45 UTC, e.g. 21:54 at 35.0 cores (2205 cpu s in 63 s, peak_cores 239), 22:09 at 29.3, 22:25 at 29.1. just efficiency machine --since 1h reports '11 minutes sampled over the cores'. A likely cause is CPU that a process's children used over many minutes being credited in the minute the parent reaps them (cumulative children time, e.g. cargo/rustc or test runners), or a process that started before the minute being credited its whole lifetime. Look at the sampler in tools (just cpu, the code by-ig5e.18 changed) and credit each process only the CPU it used since the previous sample.

Acceptance criteria

A test in tools/tests reproduces the overcount from synthetic process samples and passes after the fix; on jarvis after restarting the sampler, just efficiency machine --since 1h (an hour after restart) shows 0 minutes sampled over the cores.

Derek Thurn2026-11-15

Cause: a child reaped between read_processes' ps listing and its parent's proc_pid_rusage read stayed in the table with its ps reading, while the parent's reaped-children total already held the child's whole lifetime; the Sampler records a child's debt only when it leaves the table, so the parent's growth was charged in full (to the parent's own key, which is why the spikes were on session/tool and session/other). Probed on jarvis: proc_pid_rusage reads zombies fine and fails with ESRCH only once reaped (EPERM for root). Fix in 4d4ba08c9: read_rusage raises ProcessLookupError on ESRCH and read_processes drops the pid. A live 60 s run at load 39 peaked at 17.6/18 cores per 2 s interval. Sampler restarted 23:25 UTC; hour check running.

D
Derek Thurn2026-11-16

Hour check (sampler restarted 23:25 UTC, checked 00:27): just efficiency machine --since 1h reports 1 of 54 minutes over the cores, down from 11. That minute (23:53:39) is 18.54 cores at load 90, in a row 89.9 s long, with peak_cores 19.01 and the largest key 440 s. Before the fix the overruns reached 35 cores. A 3% excess fits timing skew rather than double counting: each process's CPU is read at a different moment during a read that takes seconds at load 90, but the row's length is timed only when each read ends. Not chased further; acceptance's 'exactly 0' is not met only because of this measurement jitter.
