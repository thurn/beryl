What exists today

  • rust/crates/beryl_engine/src/decision.rs: Decision { player, kind: DecisionKind, candidates, ask: Ask }. DecisionKind has 25 members, Ask 22 variants (11 serve one card), Opt 23. One decision is one pick. A compound question is a run of picks: a scry is ChooseUpTo picks for the bottom, then ChooseObject picks for the order on top; "you may search" is a YesNo and then ChooseUpTo picks; crew is Crew picks until the power is enough; attackers are Attacker picks and a Done.
  • The UI folds each run back into one choice and plans the picks that say it. Those files are the inventory of compound prompts: ui/src/game/choices.ts (choiceOf at about line 102 and each plan()), combat.ts (Draft), tablePick.ts, untapChoice.ts, waterbend.ts, casting.ts, decisions.ts.

Use it. Add a mark there, with its look in the data, only if a shape cannot be drawn without it.

Constraints

  1. The run-of-picks rule and the parity rule below.
  2. Decision<Small> is Copy inside the state. What replaces Ask must not make it bigger and takes no heap: plan a size assertion beside Opt's (decision.rs, size_of::<Opt<Small>>() == 4).
  3. Shape and purpose types live in beryl_card, which depends on nothing (K12). Wire types live in beryl_session's protocol, with ts-rs.
  4. Both lists are closed. The plan's shapes: pick up to N objects, order, split into destinations, yes or no, a number, a mode, a catalog choice (a color, a type, a card name), divide an amount, pay with permanents (crew, convoke and waterbend are one shape). Decide where priority, targeting and the attack and block declarations sit (shapes of their own, or purposes of the pick shape): by-w8gv.21.26 and by-w8gv.21.29 expect them to arrive as marks and buttons like any other prompt.
  5. A purpose tunes wording, sound and motion only. Two questions that are answered differently are two shapes, or one shape with a parameter.
  6. The test of the design: a new card whose question fits an existing shape and purpose needs no edit to relayout.rs, the protocol, generated/protocol.ts, the UI or tools/src/beryl_tools/xmage.py. A new purpose is one list entry and its wording.
  7. Exception 3 of docs/ui-conventions.md: never ask a yes or no before a choice the next screen can decline. A YesNo pick and the choice after it are one prompt with a way to decline (Ask::MaySearch then Ask::Search; Ask::Look).
  8. K26: the session holds a prompt's state. Each click is a command, and the answer is the marks on each object and each button's label and state. The UI decides nothing, and the phone behaves identically.

DESIGN 1 of 4: the types (settle-and-land a). Code: rust/crates/beryl_card/src/prompt.rs.

SHAPES (closed, 10): Pick, Order, Split, YesNo, Number, Mode, Catalog, Divide, Pay, Declare. The plan's nine plus Declare.

  • Priority and targeting are purposes of Pick, not shapes: both are "click an object the prompt marks", and by-w8gv.11.8 already builds them that way.
  • Attack and block declarations are a shape of their own, Declare: each object picked is paired with what it is aimed at, which no other shape has. Its state is pairs and the objects awaiting an aim, and it arrives as marks and buttons like any prompt.
  • Crew, waterbend and mana payment (plan or manual) are one shape, Pay.

PURPOSES (closed, 41). Each purpose belongs to exactly one shape: Purpose::shape() is a const fn generated from the same list entry as its name. So a decision carries a shape and a purpose, a nonsense pair cannot be written, wording keyed by purpose alone is unambiguous, and a new purpose is one list entry (Variant = "name" => Shape) plus its wording. Where today's wording differs between two uses, they are two purposes. Pick: priority, target, choose, discard, cleanup, put-on-bottom, legend-rule, look, search, untap, manifest-dread, exile-cost, combat-damage Order: trigger-order Split: scry, arrange, put-back YesNo: may, may-search, move, fateseal, top-or-bottom, explore, unless-pays, pay-life Number: x Mode: play-or-draw, mulligan, cast-offer, act (act is the session's: no decision asks it; the session opens it over a priority when the card clicked offers several ways) Catalog: color, creature-type, card-name (no card asks yet) Divide: divide-damage, distribute-counters (no card asks yet) Pay: mana, crew, waterbend Declare: attack, block

THE RECORD: Prompt { purpose: Purpose, part: Part, count: u8, most: u8, places: [Place; 2], cost: ManaCost }, 14 bytes, alignment 1, Copy, no slot and no layout in it (const assertion in prompt.rs). It lives whole in beryl_card. The one layout-dependent payload, a set of objects, is assembled around it in the kernel: Decision gets prompt: Prompt and about: Bitboard<L::Slot> in place of ask: Ask<L>.

  • Size, measured: Ask<Small> is 40 bytes (align 8), Candidates<Small> 264, Decision<Small> 312 (player 1 + kind 1 + 6 padding + 264 + 40). After: player 1 + kind 1 + Prompt 14 + about 32 + candidates 264 = 312. by-w8gv.11.3 adds const _: () = assert!(size_of::<Decision<Small>>() == 312) beside Opt's. Decision<Large> shrinks (Ask<Large> is 136).
  • relayout.rs: prompt is copied as it is; about converts as every other bitboard does. No per-prompt match.
  • The fewest fields that carry today's payloads: one or two counts -> count (what the prompt is about as a whole: cards looked at on top of a library, a crew cost's power, life to pay, an amount to divide) and most (what this part still takes: objects still to pick, power still to tap); a card or a set of objects -> about (a single card is a set of one); a player and the zone chosen from -> not stored: they are the owner and zone of the objects in about, or else of the candidates (always non-empty where they are needed); the zone moved to -> places (Place: None, Top, OnTop, Bottom, Hand, Graveyard, Exile, Battlefield; Top is "each under the one before", OnTop "each on the one before", which is the one difference between a scry's top and Jace's put-back); a mana cost -> cost.
  • "How few" is not stored: a pick that offers Opt::Done, or a run opened by a Whether, may stop at none; otherwise exactly most.
  • What about holds is fixed by shape and part, never by purpose: at Part::Whether and Part::Instead, what a yes then picks among; at Part::Aim, the objects being aimed; for YesNo and Mode, the cards the question is about; for Divide, the recipients still to be given a share; otherwise empty.
  • The cards a prompt shows: the top count of the library its objects lie in when count > 0; the whole hand or graveyard otherwise; a permanent where it lies.

HOW A PICK NAMES ITS PART: Part (1 byte): Pick (the shape's ordinary pick), Whether (yes/no: make the picks at all?), Instead (yes/no: answer with one of about in place of the most picks a no asks for), Count (a number before the picks), First and Second (a split's two destinations, in the order the engine asks for them), Aim (what the object just picked is aimed at), Plan (a whole payment at once). The kind no longer has to say it: in a scry the bottom's picks are Part::First and the top's Part::Second whatever kind they are.

RULE FOR CONSUMERS: the session folds from shape, purpose, part, the parameters, about and the candidates. It never reads the kind to decide a prompt, so a shape no card asks is testable from a decision built in a test: any kind, Opt::Number(i) candidates (a catalog's i-th entry; a share for the first recipient in about), and a Prompt of that purpose. A catalog too large for 64 candidates (card names) needs a new form of candidates when a card first asks; nothing here blocks it.

NOT CARRIED, on purpose: the eligible set of a search before its "may" is answered (Trinket Mage). The library is not looked at until the yes (CR 701.23a), so showing it would leak, and declining differs from finding nothing by the shuffle (CR 701.24a). may-search therefore stays a YesNo prompt of its own, which the session answers yes outside full control, as today (Game::searches_unasked). Exception 3 holds wherever the choice is already known: look (Whether) and discard-unless (Instead) are one prompt each.

DESIGN 4 of 4: the wire, the session and the wording (settle-and-land c, d, f). Code: rust/crates/beryl_session/src/prompt/ (view.rs the wire types, text.rs the text form, mod.rs the contract); tests/prompt.rs; ui/src/generated/protocol.ts.

C. WIRE TYPES

  • PromptView { revision, purpose, title, marks, buttons, shape }: a prompt's whole state as its seat sees it.
    • purpose is a string, Purpose::name(), not a TypeScript union: a new purpose then changes neither the protocol nor generated/protocol.ts. The UI reads no meaning from it; it keys sound and motion.
    • marks: Vec<Marked { on: Subject, mark: Mark }>, with script's Subject (a card, an ability on the stack by its script number, a player) and the closed Mark list. At most one mark on a thing: a picked card is selected, not also eligible. No new mark was needed.
    • buttons: Vec<PromptButton { id, label, enabled, primary }>. ButtonId: Confirm, Decline, Auto, PassOn, Option { index } (a candidate offered as a button: yes and no, a mode).
    • shape: ShapeView, tagged by shape, one variant per Shape (ShapeView::shape() maps back): Pick { tray, least, most, picked } | Order { items } | Split { piles: [Pile { place, label, cards, exactly, closed }] } | YesNo { about } | Number { values, value, detail } | Mode { about } | Catalog { entries, chosen } | Divide { total, left, shares } | Pay { cost, left } | Declare { arrow, pairs, aiming }.
  • The click command: ClickRequest { decision: DecisionId, revision, click } with Click: On { on: Subject } | Button { button: ButtonId } | Drop { on, pile, at } | Number { n, on } | Entry { index }. A click on an older revision is refused like an answer to an older decision.
  • ClickReply: Open { prompt } (the whole new state) | Answered (the session sent the engine its picks; the next views arrive as game-update events, as after choose).
  • REPLY, NOT A GameUpdate. A click changes nothing but the prompt, and a GameUpdate is a whole PlayerView and a script that must replay the table. The opening state rides the view (by-w8gv.11.6 and .11.8 add DecisionView.prompt), so a reloaded page takes the prompt up as it stood: current_game must return the view with the prompt's current state, not its opening one. The fixture transport replays a click's recorded replies in order, exactly as it does preview_waterbend's (ui/src/ipc.ts, beryl_cli fixture.rs): no new mechanism.
  • Not landed here, by design: the command's name constant and its entry in ipc.rs's Commands (beryl_app must register every name there), and DecisionView.prompt. by-w8gv.11.8 adds them with the first consumer. The types sit in src/prompt/ rather than protocol.rs because protocol.rs is at its file-length allowance (2465); by-w8gv.11.5 and .11.6 may move view.rs under protocol/.
  • TEXT FORM (text.rs, Display for PromptView): line 1 <shape> <purpose>: <title>; the shape's own lines; one line per mark in use (eligible #31 #40); the buttons (confirm[Find 1/2]* decline(Find Nothing): brackets when enabled, parentheses when not, a star on the primary, an option by its index). The module comment lists every line. tests/prompt.rs has one hand-written state per shape, catalog and divide built in the test.

D. SESSION-HELD STATE AND THE REST OF THE SESSION

  • Where: beside At::Human(id, Open) in game.rs. Made when next_view opens a human decision, replaced by each click, dropped by choose, cancel, undo, concede and the stand-in. It is never saved and never put in an undo point: it is a pure function of the open decision and the clicks since.
  • The choice log records engine answers only. A confirm sends the run's picks through Game::choose's path, one as each decision of the run arrives: the first Explicit, the rest Continuation. choose honours a continuation only while casting today (answer.continuation && self.casting()); the session's own run picks are continuations outside a cast too, so one undo takes back one prompt. Replay matches entries by what they chose and ignores the flag, so a save made through clicks and one made by the old UI replay alike.
  • While a run is being answered, the decisions that arrive are answered from the plan and no view is sent, as the session's automatic answers are today. A pick the engine did not ask (one answer was left, section 7.2) is skipped. A decision the plan does not expect drops the plan and opens as a prompt.
  • Undo and cancel land on a decision; its prompt is made afresh in its opening state. Cancel still restores before_cast.
  • A resumed or replayed game stops at a decision, never inside clicks, and the prompt opens afresh. An old-UI save can stop inside a run: the decision's Part says where, and the prompt opens with the earlier parts settled (a split opening at Second has its first pile closed; a pick opening after its Whether has no decline).
  • The agent's seat has no prompt state: agents answer picks. The stand-in answers the open decision's pick and drops the prompt.
  • What the opponent sees (card_handling): derived from the record with no per-purpose match: the zone of about or else of the candidates gives Library { count: the record's count, or the whole library when 0 } or Hand; its scry flag is the purpose. by-w8gv.11.3 writes it that way and tests/asks.rs must still pass.
  • set_stops redraws the view: the prompt keeps its state and a priority's pass button is relabelled.

WORDING One table in Rust, keyed by purpose, in the session: rust/crates/beryl_session/src/prompt/wording.rs (by-w8gv.11.6), a const fn wording(purpose: Purpose) -> Wording whose match is exhaustive, so a purpose without words does not compile. An entry is the title and the labels of that purpose's buttons, as templates over a closed set of holes filled from the record and the prompt's state: the counts ({picked}, {most}, {count}), {cost}, the name of the card asked about or asking, and a place's word (Place -> "Hand", "Bottom", "Exile").
