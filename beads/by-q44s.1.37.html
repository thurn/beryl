Description

Read first: docs/beryl-3d.md 'Performance gates' ('What it is held to': the stress boards). Read rust/crates/beryl_session/src/protocol/view.rs (PlayerView and ObjectView have public fields), script/mod.rs (Script, Step) and script/timing.rs (StepTiming).

A stress board is a position at the engine's capacity, built by hand with no engine. Do not use a scripted board: the engine knows 47 cards, and a board of that few names would stack into piles and cost less than a real one.

In rust/beryl3d/crates/beryl_inspect, src/stress.rs:

  • stress(name) -> Option<Stress> for stress-255 (a battlefield of 255 objects) and stress-1024 (1,024). A Stress is a list of views and, for each view after the first, its script with one StepTiming per step.
  • Every permanent has its own name (Stress 001, Stress 002, and so on). Half are creatures and half lands, a third are tapped, and they are split evenly between the two players.
  • The first view is a reset. The second moves 40 of the permanents with one fly step each, in one beat, with timings built by hand (420 ms each, 30 ms apart).
  • Everything is fixed: no clock and no random number, so two calls return equal values.

Tests in beryl_inspect/tests: two calls are equal; each board has exactly its number of objects and no two share a name; the second view's script has 40 steps and 40 timings.

Run: just b3d cargo-test -p beryl_inspect.

What the prototype found (rust/prototypes/beryl3d, 2026-11-14; read its code for a working example, then build it properly):

  • The prototype's stress boards (core/stress.rs) are a grid of n cards with 40 lanes of flights started at times fixed by a hash of lane and flight number, so the board is the same whatever the frame times.

Acceptance criteria

stress("stress-255") and stress("stress-1024") return the boards described, equal on every call, built with no engine; the tests pass.
