Close reason

partial: set steps skip no-op follows (frame 30 2.637M -> 2.627M); 2.0M not reachable by restructuring passes, two tried and rejected; designs filed as by-q44s.3.65

Description

After by-q44s.3.63 (staging fbb1fe799), beryl3d-bench Callgrind measures stress-verbs frame 30 at 2,590,489 instructions with all 106 steps compiled; about 1.9M of it is compiling the one 106-step beat in play::compile and ~0.6M is drawing. by-q44s.3.63's comments give the per-pass breakdown. Every remaining pass is a small share, so the next cut has to remove passes over the slots of each step rather than tune them. In rust/beryl3d/crates/beryl_scene/src/play/step.rs, for each step, Motion lays each dirty group into fresh (table/place.rs place_group, Placement::set), mark_affected compares every member's old and new Rest, step() walks the affected slots and calls follow (which mostly ends in retarget, updating a segment an earlier step of the same beat pushed), and commit copies every member's Rest from fresh to rests. Candidate designs: (1) one pass per dirty group that compares, follows and commits each slot while its old and new Rest are in hand, with layout writing a dense per-group list instead of the sparse Placement; (2) skip follow for a slot whose only segment since the beat began is one follow pushed, applying the retarget once when the beat ends or when anything reads or pushes the slot's track, provided the result is identical to today's tracks (same segments, causes and closing ends); (3) keep each battlefield row's members in the Index (play/index.rs) instead of scanning the battlefield per row in Zones::in_row (table/place/indexed.rs). Preserve the stress board (beryl_inspect/src/stress.rs), exact track timing and the full-placement debug comparison in Motion::commit. To attribute costs through inlining, run Callgrind in beryl3d-bench with --dump-instr=yes on b3d-frame-counts (see tools/src/beryl_tools/beryl3d/counts.py for the build and run) and map the costliest addresses with addr2line -f -i -C to the innermost frame in beryl_scene.

Acceptance criteria

On beryl3d-bench, just b3d gate --counts measures at most 2,000,000 instructions for stress-verbs frame 30 with all 106 steps compiled. The count gate, native gate and scene suite pass with measured limits. Record before and after frame-30 instructions for stress-verbs, stress-255 and stress-1024.

Measured on beryl3d-bench, staging fbb1fe799 (before this bead): stress-verbs frame 30 2,637,153 (the gate's 2,590,489 limit was set before later commits added work), stress-255 2,445,309, stress-1024 8,426,874. Where frame 30 goes, from Callgrind's own per-function self costs (fn= blocks in the dump, which are reliable): Motion::step with everything inlined into it 558K (mark_affected ~180K, commit ~75K, follow ~136K of which retarget ~69K, place), table::place::lay 248K (+ asinf 55K and sincosf 32K in the hand fan), members 85K (in_row ~65K), Table::floor 85K, Table::write 74K, memcpy 66K, FrameBuilder::finish 63K, compile 56K, malloc/free/realloc ~180K together, ObjectView clone 46K and eq 44K, SameName::of 31K.

Warning for whoever profiles next: rustc merges identical functions, so addr2line -i names a merged body after whichever function it kept. In this binary about 200K of frame 30 shows up as beryl_inspect::stress (building the board), and some as lobby or pack::sources, though none of those run in a frame. Callgrind call-site costs on '2 (recursive-looking) functions are also inflated. Trust the fn= self costs and the call counts; trust inline chains only for code that is clearly not generic.

Per-step data from stress-verbs' 106-step beat (an eprintln in Motion::step, not committed). The ten draws each lay the hand (21-30 cards) and change all of it. The twenty plays each lay the hand and one battlefield row and change 12-45 slots. The ten vanishes lay a row and change 6-29 slots. Five flips, five creature taps and three appends to your creature row each lay a 16-28 card row to change ONE card. Steps 0-4 (set) laid 121 slots and followed 104, none of which moved; this bead's commit removes those follows. Overall about 2,000 slots are laid, about 1,250 change, and follow runs about 1,200 times (about 900 of them only retarget a slide an earlier step of the beat pushed).

Two restructurings were tried and measured, and both were rejected: (1) A journal: lay straight into rests, with Placement::set noting each changed slot's old Rest, instead of laying into fresh and then comparing and copying. Frame 30 went UP to 2,835,714: the per-set bookkeeping (stamp, Rest compare, push of the old Rest) and a removal pass cost 236K, more than mark_affected plus commit saved. (2) One pass that swaps each laid slot's Rest between rests and fresh (so fresh keeps the old one for before) while marking, which removes commit's copy. Frame 30 went to 2,711,977: the swap closure did not inline and cost 96 instructions a slot, and the pass as a whole cost what mark_affected and commit did. Lesson: the per-slot passes are already tight. Their cost is moving 50-byte Rests and comparing Poses, not the number of passes. 2.0M needs fewer slots laid and followed, not cheaper passes.
