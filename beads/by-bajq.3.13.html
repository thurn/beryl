Description

Why

Griselbrand (by-bajq.3.8) has "Pay 7 life: Draw seven cards." CostPart::Life(u8) exists in the vocabulary (beryl_card/src/cost.rs) and renders as "pay N life", but the engine cannot pay it: payable::check (beryl_kernel/src/payable.rs) refuses any card with a CostPart::Life (its test card Bloodletter is refused with Unpayable::Part(CostPart::Life(2))), and cast/parts.rs treats the part as unreachable. Without this, the Griselbrand session can only escalate the card. Force of Will's alternative cost (by-bajq.7.2) and Teferi's Protection's "a cost of life can't be paid" (by-bajq.8.4, CR 119.8) build on the same part.

What to build

  • Paying life as part of a cost (CR 119.4): a player may pay an amount of life greater than 0 only if their life total is at least that amount. A player may always pay 0 life.
  • payable::check accepts CostPart::Life. The legality test for activating or casting (cast/legal.rs, cast/costs.rs, and whatever lists the actions offered to a player) refuses a cost whose life the player cannot pay, so at 6 life Griselbrand's ability is not offered and the AI never sees it.
  • cast/parts.rs pays it as the costs are paid (CR 601.2h, 602.2b): the player loses that much life through the existing life mutator (State::lose_life, beryl_kernel/src/player.rs), so it is an ordinary life loss for triggers and trackers. Keep the CostPart::OneOf arm as it is.
  • Leave a single place that asks "can this player pay N life" so by-bajq.8.4's rule change ("your life total can't change") has one function to amend.

Tests

A unit test in beryl_rules/src/tests/ with a test-only definition (Bloodletter in payable.rs is one): the ability is offered at exactly N life and paid, leaving 0; it is not offered at N-1; the life loss is visible to a "whenever a player loses life" listener if one exists. Update payable.rs's own test that expects Bloodletter to be refused.

Files

beryl_kernel/src/payable.rs, beryl_rules/src/cast/{parts.rs, legal.rs, costs.rs}, beryl_kernel/src/player.rs, beryl_rules/src/tests/. Contract text in beryl_rules/src/lib.rs if it lists the cost parts the engine pays.

  • size_of::<State<Small>>() stays within 20,480 bytes. Report the size if you changed it.
  • The frozen saves replay and rollouts_never_change holds (just test-rust). A change to what the WU mirror's rollouts do is a bug in this epic.

  • A new card follows the card-writing skill: just card new, just card check <name> (Beryl, XMage and Forge), every instruction carried out by its own scenarios, and just specs <card> passing the generic UI checks from both seats. A disagreement with an oracle is settled by the rules and recorded in data/oracle-divergences.json.
  • Read the card's rulings before writing its scenarios (just card brief <name> prints Scryfall's). Each statement in a ruling that a scenario can show is a scenario of the card. Where one cannot be shown yet, the close reason names the ruling and why.
  • A rules bug found on the way gets its docs/bugs.md entry.
  • If the design in the plan is wrong for the code, build what is right and amend the plan's section in the same push. Never wait for an answer: record the choice in this bead.

Acceptance criteria

Handoff: Griselbrand's session (1e8e8a91) had already done most of this bead: payable::pays_for_ability accepts CostPart::Life, cast/legal.rs refuses an ability whose life the player cannot pay (gated on Cost::pays_life_or_chooses so the WU mirror pays nothing), and cast/parts.rs pays it via life::lose. This bead (1a8a54c3) adds State::can_pay_life (beryl_kernel/src/player.rs), the single predicate CR 119.8 / by-bajq.8.4 should amend; it also allows paying 0 life at any total (CR 119.4b). parts::life_payable now calls it. New tests in beryl_rules/src/tests/life_costs.rs: not offered at 2 life for 'Pay 3 life', paid at 3 leaving 0 with Event::LifeLoss, game lost to SBA; can_pay_life(0) at negative life. Not done here: pays_for_spell still refuses CostPart::Life, so a spell's life cost (Force of Will alt cost, by-bajq.7.2) needs that bead to add it and pay it in the spell-cost path. No 'whenever a player loses life' trigger exists, so the test checks the LifeLoss event instead.
