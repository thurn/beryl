Beryl 3D record step is killed at the fast check's 180 s limit

  • Description
  • Comments2
  • Changelog

Description

Symptom

Every Beryl 3D fast-check lane but beryl3d starts with the record step (cli.record: the shared test-profile build cli.SHARED_BUILD, then the record program that builds the recordings pack). Between 16:10 and 18:10 UTC on 2026-11-16, record itself was killed at the 180 s hard limit 12 times across the three lanes (runs.jsonl, script check, lanes beryl3d-*, records with timeout), and it took 40-180 s when it finished. In the first fast check of commit e3ac0c403 (18:25 UTC), all three record steps were killed, so no Beryl 3D test ran at all. When it is killed, every later step of its lane is deferred to the Beryl 3D sweep.

In a fresh worktree whose Beryl 3D target was cloned from $BERYL_CACHE/b3d/target, refreshed within the hour ("warm: cloned ... in 7.4 s, gave 2429 unchanged files the times of the build it cloned"), just b3d record took 271 s wall and 405 CPU-seconds at load 15. So it compiled a lot, although the cache was fresh. A second run took 1.9 s.

To find out

  1. Which crates the first build compiles, and why: CARGO_LOG=cargo::core::compiler::fingerprint=info on a fresh worktree's first just b3d record, or cargo build --timings with the same flags as cli.SHARED_BUILD. Candidates: the cache is saved from a build with other features or profile than SHARED_BUILD (the sweep's steps or clippy's target), path dependencies on the main workspace's crates whose mtimes warm.py does not restamp, or build scripts that rerun.
  2. How much of the wall time is waiting rather than compiling: the cargo shim's lease (tools/src/beryl_tools/cargo_pool.py; leases.jsonl lines with command cargo build and record, waited_s, cores) and macOS's first-run check of new binaries (cli.linked explains it for b3d; record is run through cli.program, which is the copied file in target/debug, so it pays the check after every link).
  3. Whether the three lanes' record steps really share one build (paths.build_lock), or each waits its own turn for the lease after the lock.

Done

Over a day of Beryl 3D fast checks, record is killed in under 5% of runs and finishes in under 60 s at the median. Files: tools/src/beryl_tools/beryl3d/cli.py (record, shared_build, build, warm_target), tools/src/beryl_tools/warm.py, tools/src/beryl_tools/beryl3d/sweep.py (who saves $BERYL_CACHE/b3d/target), tools/src/beryl_tools/cargo_pool.py.

Derek Thurn2026-11-16

Run of 2026-11-16 20:05 UTC (CI structural review), measured from runs.jsonl hour files, fast checks on this Mac. The record step's build costs about the same CPU in every push: one lane's record step uses 130-300 CPU-seconds, and the other two lanes wait on the build lock with under 1 CPU-second each. Between 18:00 and 19:00 UTC, those builds took 85-180 s of wall time, and record was killed at 180 s 13 times. In five of those runs, all three lanes had used under 1 CPU-second at the kill, so nothing in the push was compiling. Between 19:00 and 20:10 UTC, builds of the same 130-200 CPU-seconds took 20-50 s, record's median was 24 s, and no record step was killed. Agent-class leases fell from about 10 to 6.5 core-hours an hour over the same span. So the cache is not the main cause. The time goes to waiting for CPU tokens, and leases.jsonl did not show those waits, because only released leases wrote a line. Commit b0328a39c now logs waits that never get a lease (outcome abandoned or gave_up, cores 0). Next: count the abandoned push waits over a day and find out which leases held the tokens at those moments.

So the record step's 130-500 CPU-seconds are mostly the distance from the slot's last build to staging's tip. Filed as by-ihm4r with options. Separately, 8 of 228 Beryl 3D fast checks since 12:00 UTC 2026-11-16 had record killed at 180 s with under 3 CPU-seconds in every lane and no lease line at all (for example slot-10, tree 3c67e07d, 02:00 UTC 2026-11-17): something other than the token pool, probably the checkout's build lock, held them.
