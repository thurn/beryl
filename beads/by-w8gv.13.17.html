Close reason

Bench, scale, nightly and docs now measure under normal load; quiet-machine refusals removed

Description

The development Mac runs many agent sessions and slow CI at once; a load of 20 to 40 on its 18 cores is normal (user, 2026-11-12: 'This machine is always under heavy load we need to change any plan which assumes that is not normal'). Several plans assume otherwise: tools/src/beryl_tools/scale.py's docstring says --assert is 'for a rehearsal on a quiet machine' and the nightly passes only --assert-memory for that reason; docs/benchmarking.md says to confirm with interleaved timing 'on a quiet Mac', and 'A quiet machine' has just bench refuse to time when more than 10% of the CPU is busy or a compiler runs, which on this machine is always; docs/engine-architecture.md (around line 1265) says timing waits for a human on a quiet machine. Rework them so measurements are made under normal load: interleave old and new (ABBA) with enough rounds and report medians and spread, prefer instruction counts and CPU-seconds, use the load-adjusted clock (tools/src/beryl_tools/clock.py), and decide whether the scale rehearsal's time budgets are asserted under load (and how) instead of being reserved for a quiet run. Update the docs, just bench's quiet-machine refusal (or make it advisory), and scale.py's docstring and nightly use together.

Acceptance criteria

No doc, check or tool in the repo requires or waits for a quiet machine; just bench times under normal load with interleaving and reports its spread; scale.py and docs/benchmarking.md describe how to read results taken under load.

Derek Thurn2026-11-13

Decisions: (1) just bench no longer refuses on CPU load or running builds; it logs CPU %, busiest processes and builds before and after timing, and refuses (without --force) only on battery, Low Power Mode or throttling, which slow both sides for the whole run. Verified by hand at 65-88% CPU busy with cargo running: example_abba --quick gave B/A 0.6147 (95% CI 0.5996-0.6261), as tight as the quiet-machine example in the doc. (2) Scale time budgets stay unasserted in the nightly: under normal load the load-adjusted times still spread more than their margins (cold check 41-56 s vs 60; rebuild medians 2.2-3.2 s vs 2), and a nightly failure files a Steward bead. --assert is the manual judge, run under normal load with the new --edits N (median, range printed); compare changes by alternating A B B A runs. (3) The nightly's load skip moved from 2x cores (inside the normal 20-40 range on 18 cores) to clock.MAX_STRETCH (8x), where deadlines stop stretching.
