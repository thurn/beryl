Description

Read first: docs/beryl-3d.md, all of it once, then closely 'Components', 'The frame', 'Determinism', 'The table', 'Rendering' (The Baseline tier), 'Card faces' and 'Threads'; the epic's decisions and its 'Code map'.

Build the smallest program that shows whether Beryl 3D's basic ideas work, feel right and are fast: a real game, played by the real session, drawn as a 3D table in a window. It is a prototype. Milestone 1 builds the real thing and this is deleted then, so favor a working whole over finish, and do not build what is listed under 'Leave out'.

Where it lives: a standalone Cargo package at rust/prototypes/beryl3d with its own empty [workspace] table, so it is outside the main workspace and adds nothing to the fast check or slow CI. It depends on beryl_session and beryl_search by path, and on wgpu 30, winit 0.30, glam and an image decoder. Check wgpu 30's and glam's current documentation before writing against them: both changed their APIs this year. Keep each file under 800 lines.

Keep the doc's central split even here, because it is one of the ideas on trial: a core module with no GPU, clock or thread in it that turns session updates and a time into a list of 64-byte draw records, and a render module that draws the list.

What it does:

  • Starts a real game through beryl_session::Sessions::start_game, the WU Control mirror against the mcts-hidden agent, with a stand_in agent (expert) answering the human seat, so the game plays itself while real search threads run. Each GameUpdate goes down a channel to the window's thread. Read rust/crates/beryl_app/src/games.rs and lib.rs for how the Tauri shell builds Sessions and its emitter.
  • Places every object of the view in 3D: both battlefields in rows, both hands (the viewer's fanned at the bottom, tilted toward the camera), the stack, and the library, graveyard and exile as piles with real height. Millimetre units, the table at z = 0, one fixed perspective camera. For where zones stand, look at ui/src/game/battlefield.ts and handLayout.ts; for how the camera should look, film Arena with the arena-video skill and match its tilt by eye.
  • Plays each view's script: fly as an arc through the air with the card turning over where its face changes, appear and vanish as fades, tap as a rotation, and every other verb as an instant change. Durations and curves come from data/ui-conventions.json. Positions are functions of time (the doc's Segment and pose_at), never accumulated.
  • Draws as the doc's Baseline tier says: cards as rounded-rectangle meshes and the table, opaque, nearest first, with a depth buffer; then one blurred shadow quad under each card that is above the table, blended. One instanced draw per pass. Card faces are layers of one array texture.
  • Card faces are the Scryfall scans already in the app's image cache (find the cache directory where beryl_app sets up art), decoded at startup and scaled to 256 by 360. Uncompressed is fine here. A face with no cached scan is a flat color with a stripe. Never download anything.
  • Pointer: the card of the viewer's hand under the pointer lifts and enlarges, by ray against the card's plane.
  • Draws only when something animates or the pointer moves. Asks the display for its highest refresh rate.
  • Keys: 1 for the live game, 2 and 3 for stress boards of 255 and 1,024 cards in which 40 cards at a time fly between random places, Space to pause, S to print the statistics below to the terminal.
  • The window's title shows the time the last frame took on the CPU, the count of late frames, and whether the AI is searching.

With no window (--stats live|stress-255|stress-1024 [--frames N], --png <ms> <path>): the same core and renderer draw into a texture, and the program prints, as the median and the worst of five runs with the load average beside them:

  • nanoseconds per frame in the core, and nanoseconds to encode and submit;
  • heap allocations per frame in the core after the first second (a counting global allocator); the target is zero;
  • draw records, draw calls, and the screen area of the opaque and of the blended records in screens;
  • the same frame drawn twice, once as built and once with every record blended back to front and no depth buffer, with the GPU time of each if wgpu's timestamp queries work on this Mac;
  • peak memory, the stripped executable's size, and the time from start to first frame;
  • whether everything draws on a device created with wgpu::Limits::downlevel_webgl2_defaults();
  • for live: the longest frame while the AI was searching against the longest while it was not.

just proto3d (and its by wrapper) opens the window and refuses to run for an agent, as just dev does (see tools/src/beryl_tools/app.py). just proto3d --stats ... and --png ... run for anyone. You cannot open the window yourself: check your work with --png at several moments of the live game and of each stress board, and look at the pictures.

Leave out: text of any kind, prompts and clicking to play, the flat layer, sound, marks, arrows, texture compression, the Enhanced tier, phones, Windows, tests beyond what you need to trust the numbers, and any check or lint.

When it runs:

  • Comment on the epic by-q44s with the full statistics for all three boards, each beside the doc's budget for it ('Performance gates', 'Rendering', 'Card faces', 'Threads'), and three short lists: what the prototype confirmed, what it refuted, what it could not test (the floor's GPU is one: say plainly that this Mac cannot show it).
  • Correct docs/beryl-3d.md where a claim proved wrong, and replace the first two prototypes' numbers in its 'Measurements' section with this one's.
  • Correct the descriptions of milestone 1's beads (by-q44s.1.*) where what you learned changes them: an API that differs from what a bead assumes, a step that was harder or easier than the bead says, a file it should name.
  • Say in your final message how to run it and what to look for.

Run: just proto3d --stats live; just proto3d --stats stress-255; just proto3d --stats stress-1024; just proto3d --png 5000 <path>.

Acceptance criteria

just proto3d opens a window where a real game plays itself in 3D with card images, flights with shadows and a hand that lifts under the pointer; the three --stats runs print every number listed; the epic carries the numbers against the doc's budgets and the confirmed, refuted and untested lists; milestone 1's beads and the doc are corrected.
