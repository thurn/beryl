Windows promotes master past the Mac-only lanes

  • Close reason
  • Description
  • Changelog

Close reason

Fixed in 54a5534c3, which passed slow CI; master advanced past it. I took the bead's alternative fix instead of gating Enact's promotion. A lane that Windows skips now keeps its last Mac pass as its base even when that pass is older than master (slow_ci.Passes.base, only_here). bisect.start searches a failed lane of that kind from that pass, so its culprit can be found. Promotion is unchanged: a Windows pass still moves master, so a break on a Mac-only lane can still reach master. Gating promotion would stall master whenever the Mac is busy, and Beryl's worktrees branch from staging, not master.

Description

What someone sees: slow CI on this Mac fails a Mac-only lane (count-gate, oracle, or the motion and ui-smoke verb checks), and bisection prints "fails at <tip> too: no commit since master to blame". The Steward bead then has no culprit, and the failure is already on master. Example: on 2026-11-15 at 36cf4c73, count-gate failed at +1.38% over its bar with no commit to blame (Steward by-ufe4).

Why: a slow-CI pass on either machine moves master forward. Cygnus (Windows) skips app, ui-smoke, oracle and counts (WINDOWS_SKIPS in tools/src/beryl_tools/checks.py). When Cygnus passes a tip before the Mac has tested it, master moves past commits that no machine has checked on those lanes. The Mac's bisection searches only the commits since master, so the culprit is outside its range. .git/enact/ci.log holds 56 "no commit since master to blame" bisections. The most frequent are count-gate (8), oracle:Generated (6), oracle:Rest in Peace (4) and the verb:vanish and verb:appear motion checks (3 each per seat and layout).

Fix: promote a tip to master only when every lane has passed at that tip on some host. A lane that one OS skips must pass on the other. $BERYL_CACHE/ci/lane-passes.json already records lane passes per tree, and slow_ci.py writes it. Enact's promotion is in ~/enact/scripts/enact.py, around the ci-promotion.json handling near line 3358; make the change through Enact's own process. An alternative is to have bisection search from the last tip where that lane passed rather than from master. Either one makes the culprit findable.

Done when: a Windows pass on a tip the Mac has not tested leaves master where it is until the Mac-only lanes pass, there is a test of that rule in Enact's suite, and the count of "no commit since master to blame" lines in a week of ci.log drops to the cases where the failure really predates master.
