Delivered discounted rollout scores and four-level search branches with four recorded suite runs

Description

Part of the ladder epic by-97g. Read docs/ai-strategy.md first: §2.2 (the clear leads are not the rollouts') and §4.7.

just why (by-97g.20, rust/crates/beryl_cli/src/why.rs) plays paired rollouts after two candidates and reports raw win rates. On four suite entries the search prefers the wrong play by 0.02 to 0.07 and those rollouts show no such preference. So the preference arises somewhere just why cannot see: in how the search scores a rollout (the turn discount) or in the tree below the move. This bead makes it show both. It needs judgement and is not tier:light.

Where

  • rust/crates/beryl_cli/src/why.rs: compare, play, deal.
  • rust/crates/beryl_search/src/long.rs:
    • value and discounted: how the search scores a finished game (TURN_DISCOUNT), counted in turns from the tree's root (Tree::turns).
    • LongSearch::choose, plant, grow: how a decision is searched.
    • Tree, Node (key, visits, reward, mover, first, sibling), Tree::child, keys: a tree's nodes are keyed by a hash of the candidate, the same key in every world, so a node's children are told apart by keys of the decision reached in that tree's own world.
  • rust/crates/beryl_cli/src/blunders.rs: reach, and how the suite builds the agent for an entry at a budget and seed.
  • rust/crates/beryl_cli/src/transcript.rs: labels, label.

Build

  1. The search's own score. In the existing report, under the win rates, print for A and for B: the mean number of turns from the position to the game's end, and the mean score as the search counts it (a win 0.5 + 0.5 * TURN_DISCOUNT^turns, a loss 0.5 - 0.5 * TURN_DISCOUNT^turns, a draw 0.5, for the acting player), and A − B of that score with its paired standard error. Make the search's scoring function public; do not copy its formula.
  2. --family FAMILY. The rollout policy is that family's (Family::rollout_policy) and, with --tree, so is the search. Default: the champion (DEEP_SEARCHER).
  3. --tree [--work 3M] [--seed 0]. After the rollout report, search the entry's position once with that family at that budget and seed, as the suite does, and print the tree below A and below B, summed over all the trees:
    • one line for the candidate itself: its visits and its mean;
    • then four levels down. At each level, the decision reached (player and kind, and in how many trees it was reached), and every child of it: its label, its visits and its mean. Go down through the child with the most visits.
    • Every mean is printed for the player acting at the root, whoever moves.
    • A child's label comes from the decision in the tree's own world (labels); sum children across trees by label. Add what is needed to LongSearch as one public method that returns plain data (labels are made in beryl_cli). It must not change how a search plays.

Tests

  • With A and B the same label, the scores and turns are equal.
  • --tree on oust-own-token with --a "cast Oust" --b pass --work 100k prints both candidates, and each level's children's visits sum to no more than their parent's.
  • A search run for --tree chooses as the same search does without it (same family, budget and seed).
  • just test-rust, just check.

Done

Pushed with enact push. On Cygnus, run these and paste, for each, the score line and the four levels below A:

  • just why beza-trinket-into-blockers --a "attack with Beza, the Bounding Spring" --b done --tree
  • just why manifest-into-samurai --a "attack with face-down Spara's Headquarters" --b done --tree

  • just why skip-teferi-plus-one --a "Teferi, Hero of Dominaria +1" --b pass --tree

Then write one sentence for each: is the search's lead explained by the score (the turns differ), by a node below the move whose children are spread across plays a player would not make, or by neither.
