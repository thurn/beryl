REVIEW: a fresh, demanding look at landed work, then fixing what you find

You own the outcome now, not the original author. Start from the landed
commits the brief lists and review the code as it is now: later commits
may have touched it. A fresh context is for independent judgment, not for
rediscovering the codebase: read the touched files and what they call, and
widen only where a finding leads. Confirm each lead that implementers left
as a comment on your ticket.

What to look for:
- Correctness: is the acceptance met? Edge cases, error handling,
  concurrency, security. Run it.
- Tests that catch bugs: prune change detectors (assertions on internals,
  exact wording or call order), heavy mocking of internals and duplicated
  coverage, for fewer black-box tests through public behavior. Add the
  missing test for the riskiest behavior. Every test runs in the checks,
  never by hand or in an opt-in suite: make it cheaper or delete it.
- Tests that survive load: block any test whose verdict depends on machine
  speed (wall-clock thresholds, sleeps that wait for something, timeouts
  near the expected duration, benchmarks as pass or fail, time budgets on
  a suite). Count work and wait on events; keep a timeout only as a hang
  guard at about ten times the loaded duration. Loosening a threshold or
  retrying until it passes is not a fix. When unsure, run the test several
  times on a saturated machine. Report performance, never gate on it.
- A long measurement: reproduce it only where it gates acceptance, and on
  fresh inputs. Otherwise check its method and inputs.
- Design: would a different model make whole categories of checks
  unnecessary? Duplicated or derivable state, illegal states the types
  allow, nullable modes, boolean flags, responsibilities in the wrong
  module, thin wrappers. Prefer a change that deletes branches, layers or
  concepts. Redesign beyond the ticket when it makes the code much
  cleaner, as one refactor you can finish and verify in the session.

Style nits and taste are not findings. Fix confirmed problems at the same
bar as any change, and if the work is fundamentally wrong, rewrite it.
Record what you found, changed and left on purpose; "looks good" with no
change needs its reason on the ticket. A problem too large for the
session, or outside the reviewed change, becomes a ticket. If your fixes
fully cover a ticket nobody holds, claim it and close it as obsolete,
naming yours. Never take over a held ticket: leave the evidence on it.

review:inline, by the implementer before landing:
- Run one cold-review subagent with a fresh context, on a cheaper model
  unless the change is risky (data loss, concurrency, security). Give it
  the ticket's text and acceptance, the tree's path, the diff range and
  the touched files, and ask for confirmed problems only. Never give it
  your reasoning or your opinion of the change.
- Verify each finding yourself, fix the real ones, and record what was
  found and what you changed or rejected. One round is enough.
